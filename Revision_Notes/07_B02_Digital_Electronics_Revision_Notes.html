📖 Chapter 1: Number Systems & Codes: Radix Conversions, 1's

20 Questions in Booklet
📌 1.1 Radix Conversions, 1's & 2's Complement Ranges & Signed Overflow Logic
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 n-bit 2's Complement Range: −2^(n−1) to +(2^(n−1) − 1)
  • 🔹 1's Complement / Sign-Magnitude Range: −(2^(n−1) − 1) to +(2^(n−1) − 1)
  • 🔹 Overflow Flag V = C_in ⊕ C_out
Simple Exam Points
  • In an n-bit register, Sign-Magnitude and 1's Complement suffer from two representations of zero (+0 and −0), whereas 2's Complement has a unique zero and one extra negative number (−2^(n−1)
  • e.g., −128 to +127 for 8-bit, −32768 to +32767 for 16-bit).
  • Signed arithmetic overflow NEVER occurs when adding two numbers of opposite signs
  • it occurs only when adding two positives yields MSB=1 or adding two negatives yields MSB=0, detected in hardware by XORing the carry into the sign bit (C_n-1) with the carry out of the sign bit (C_n).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: Convert (10110.101)_2 to Decimal -> 16 + 0 + 4 + 2 + 0 + 0.5 + 0 + 0.125 = 22.625. In 8-bit 2's complement, −45 is: +45 = 00101101 -> keep rightmost '1' and invert left bits -> 11010011.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: 2's complement nikalne ki 2-second trick: Rightmost '1' tak saare bits (trailing zeros aur pehla 1) same to same utaar do, aur uske baaye (left) ke saare bits पलट (invert) do!
📌 1.2 BCD (8421), Excess-3 Self-Complementing Code, Gray Code & Hamming SECDED
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Binary-to-Gray: G_n = B_n, G_i = B_(i+1) ⊕ B_i
  • 🔹 Hamming Parity Bits p: 2^p >= m + p + 1
  • 🔹 Min Hamming Distance for d-bit detection = d + 1; for t-bit correction = 2t + 1
Simple Exam Points
  • BCD (8421) is a weighted code (valid 0000 to 1001
  • invalid 1010 to 1111 — add 0110 = 6 whenever nibble sum > 9 or carry out = 1).
  • Excess-3 (BCD + 0011) and 2421 are SELF-COMPLEMENTING codes (inverting all bits gives the 9's complement of the decimal digit!).
  • Gray Code is an unweighted unit-distance cyclic code (only 1 bit changes between adjacent values), essential in rotary shaft encoders and K-map headers to eliminate transient race glitches.
  • Hamming Code places parity bits at power-of-2 bit positions (1, 2, 4, 8, 16...).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Hamming Math: How many parity bits p are needed to protect m = 11 data bits against single-bit errors? Check 2^p >= 11 + p + 1: For p=3, 8 < 15 (No); For p=4, 2^4 = 16 >= 11 + 4 + 1 = 16 (Yes! p = 4 parity bits, total 15-bit Hamming codeword).
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: Excess-3 aur 2421 'Self-Complementing Codes' hain (9's complement = 1's complement), jabki 8421 BCD self-complementing NAHI hai! Aur Gray code unweighted unit-distance code hai.

📖 Chapter 2: Boolean Algebra & Gates — Boolean Algebra Laws, De Morgan's Theorem, Duality & Consensus Theorem

17 Questions in Booklet
📌 2.1 Boolean Algebra Laws, De Morgan's Theorem, Duality & Consensus Theorem
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Consensus Theorem: AB + A'C + BC = AB + A'C (Redundant term BC eliminated)
  • 🔹 Distributive: A + BC = (A + B)(A + C)
  • 🔹 Absorption: A + A'B = A + B
Simple Exam Points
  • To find the Dual of any Boolean expression: interchange OR (+) and AND (·), and interchange 0 and 1, while keeping variables and their complements unchanged!
  • To find the Complement (De Morgan's Law): take the Dual AND complement every literal ((A+B)' = A'·B' and (A·B)' = A'+B').
  • For n Boolean variables, there are 2^n minterms/maxterms and 2^(2^n) possible distinct Boolean functions (for n=2, 16 functions
  • for n=3, 256 functions
  • for n=4, 65,536 functions).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Universal Gate Realization Counts (Minimum 2-input gates required): [NOT: 1 NAND, 1 NOR], [AND: 2 NAND, 3 NOR], [OR: 3 NAND, 2 NOR], [XOR: 4 NAND, 5 NOR], [XNOR: 5 NAND, 4 NOR], [Half Adder: 5 NAND, 5 NOR], [Full Adder: 9 NAND, 9 NOR].
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: Dual nikalte waqt variables ko complement MAT karna (sirf + <-> · aur 0 <-> 1 badalna)! Aur Consensus Theorem me jab A aur A' dikhein (AB + A'C + BC), to teesra term BC hamesha gayab (redundant) ho jata hai!

📖 Chapter 3: K-Maps & Minimization — Karnaugh Maps (K-Maps), Prime Implicants, Essential PIs & Don't Care (X) Conditions

19 Questions in Booklet
📌 3.1 Karnaugh Maps (K-Maps), Prime Implicants, Essential PIs & Don't Care (X) Conditions
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Group of 2^k adjacent cells in an n-variable K-Map eliminates k variables and leaves (n − k) literals in the product/sum term
Simple Exam Points
  • K-Map adjacency follows Gray Code order (00, 01, 11, 10) with wrap-around edges and four corners forming a valid 4-cell quad.
  • A Prime Implicant (PI) is any maximal valid power-of-2 group (1, 2, 4, 8, 16) that cannot be completely contained inside a larger valid group.
  • An Essential Prime Implicant (EPI) is a Prime Implicant that covers at least ONE minterm ('1') not covered by any other Prime Implicant.
  • Don't Care ('X' or 'd') cells are included ONLY if they help enlarge a group of 1s (or 0s in POS)
  • never form a group containing only X's.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Minterm vs Maxterm Relationship: For any n-variable function, if F(A,B,C) = Σm(1, 3, 5, 7), then its POS form uses the remaining indices: F(A,B,C) = ΠM(0, 2, 4, 6), and its complement F' = Σm(0, 2, 4, 6).
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: n-variable K-map me 2^k cells ka group k variables ko hata deta hai aur (n − k) variables बचते hain! Jaise 4-variable K-map me Octet (8 = 2³ cells) 4 − 3 = 1 literal deta hai!

📖 Chapter 4: Combinational Circuits — Combinational Circuits — Half/Full Adders, Carry Look-Ahead, MUX & Decoders

📌 4.1 Combinational Circuits — Half/Full Adders, Carry Look-Ahead, MUX & Decoders
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Full Adder: Sum = A ⊕ B ⊕ C_in; Carry C_out = AB + BC_in + AC_in = AB + C_in(A ⊕ B)
  • 🔹 Carry Look-Ahead: G_i = A_i·B_i (Generate), P_i = A_i ⊕ B_i (Propagate)
Simple Exam Points
  • A Full Adder can be built using 2 Half Adders and 1 OR gate (or 9 two-input NAND/NOR gates).
  • A Ripple Carry Adder has O(n) propagation delay (n × t_carry), whereas a Carry Look-Ahead (CLA) Adder computes carries in parallel using C_(i+1) = G_i + P_i·C_i in constant O(1) gate delay.
  • A 2^n : 1 Multiplexer (Data Selector) has 2^n data inputs, n select lines, and 1 output
  • any n-variable Boolean function can be implemented using a 2^(n−1) : 1 MUX (plus an inverter) without extra gates!
  • An n : 2^n Decoder with OR gate is a minterm generator.
✅ Direct Exam Example: MUX Tree Formula: To build a large N : 1 MUX using smaller k : 1 MUXes, divide repeatedly by k until quotient is 1 and sum the quotients. E.g., 64:1 MUX using 4:1 MUXes = 64/4 + 16/4 + 4/4 = 16 + 4 + 1 = 21 MUXes.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: Kisi bhi 4-variable Boolean function ko bina kisi extra logic gate ke implement karne ke liye 8:1 MUX (2^(n−1) : 1 MUX) kaafi hota hai (4th variable data input lines par 0, 1, D, D' ke roop me jata hai)!

📖 Chapter 5: Flip-Flops & Latches — Sequential Circuits — SR, JK, D & T Flip-Flops, Characteristic Equations & Race-Around

13 Questions in Booklet
📌 5.1 Sequential Circuits — SR, JK, D & T Flip-Flops, Characteristic Equations & Race-Around
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Characteristic Equations: SR: Q(t+1) = S + R'Q (SR=0)
  • 🔹 JK: Q(t+1) = JQ' + K'Q
  • 🔹 D: Q(t+1) = D
  • 🔹 T: Q(t+1) = T ⊕ Q = TQ' + T'Q
Simple Exam Points
  • Latches are level-triggered (transparent during active enable level), whereas Flip-Flops are edge-triggered (respond only at rising ↑ or falling ↓ clock transitions).
  • In an SR Flip-Flop, S=1, R=1 is the Forbidden/Invalid state.
  • The JK Flip-Flop resolves this by toggling (Q(t+1) = Q'(t)) when J=1, K=1.
  • However, in a level-triggered JK circuit, if J=1, K=1 and clock pulse width t_p > propagation delay t_pd, the output oscillates multiple times within a single clock pulse — known as the Race-Around Condition!
  • It is eliminated by (1) Edge-triggering, or (2) Master-Slave JK Flip-Flop.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Excitation Table Quick Lookup (Q_n -> Q_(n+1)): For JK FF: [0->0: J=0, K=X], [0->1: J=1, K=X], [1->0: J=X, K=1], [1->1: J=X, K=0].
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: Race-Around Condition sirf LEVEL-triggered JK Latch me tab hoti hai jab J=1, K=1 ho aur Clock Pulse Width (t_p) Flip-Flop delay (t_pd) se BADI ho (t_p > t_pd)!

📖 Chapter 6: Counters & Registers — Ripple vs Synchronous Counters, Ring/Johnson Counters & Shift Registers

12 Questions in Booklet
📌 6.1 Ripple vs Synchronous Counters, Ring/Johnson Counters & Shift Registers
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 n-Flip-Flop Max Modulus = 2^n
  • 🔹 Ring Counter Mod = n (Self-correcting? No)
  • 🔹 Johnson (Twisted Ring / Mobius) Counter Mod = 2n
  • 🔹 Output Freq f_out = f_clk / Mod
Simple Exam Points
  • In an Asynchronous (Ripple) Counter of n flip-flops, the output of one FF clocks the next FF, resulting in cumulative propagation delay N × t_pd (limiting max clock frequency to f_max = 1 / (n · t_pd)).
  • In a Synchronous Counter, all FFs share a common clock pulse simultaneously, so f_max = 1 / (t_pd + t_gate).
  • Shift Registers (SISO, SIPO, PISO, PIPO): To serially load an n-bit SISO register takes n clock pulses, and to read it out serially takes (n − 1) or n additional pulses.
  • Shifting a binary number LEFT by k bits multiplies by 2^k
  • shifting RIGHT by k bits divides by 2^k.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Counter Math: A 4-bit ripple counter uses flip-flops with propagation delay t_pd = 25 ns each. Total worst-case delay = 4 × 25 = 100 ns -> Maximum reliable clock frequency f_max = 1 / 100 ns = 10 MHz. If clocked at 16 MHz, output frequency of MSB = 16 MHz / 16 = 1 MHz.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: n Flip-Flops se Ring Counter me Mod = n states milte hain (2^n − n unused), jabki Johnson (Twisted Ring) Counter me Mod = 2n states milte hain! Aur 3-stage MOD-4, MOD-5, MOD-6 cascaded counters ka total MOD = 4 × 5 × 6 = 120 hota hai!

📝 Chapter 6 — Practice Questions & Simple Explanations

Q6.1: Among the four types of 4-bit Shift Registers (SISO, SIPO, PISO, PIPO), which is the FASTEST and which is the SLOWEST for loading and reading 4 bits?
(A) SISO is the fastest and PIPO is the slowest
(B) SIPO is faster than PIPO
(C) PIPO is the fastest; SISO is the slowest ✅
(D) All four take 4 clock cycles
Answer (C): PIPO is the fastest (1 clock cycle); SISO is the slowest (`2n − 1` or `n + n` clock cycles) — PIPO (Parallel-In Parallel-Out) loads in 1 clock pulse and reads in 0 additional pulses; SISO (Serial-In Serial-Out) takes `n` pulses to shift in and `n − 1` (or `n`) pulses to shift out.
Q6.2: Shifting a binary number Stored in a register by 1 bit position to the LEFT (inserting 0 at LSB) is equivalent to:
(A) Dividing the number by 2
(B) Adding 1 to the number
(C) Complementing the number
(D) Multiplying the number by 2 ✅
Answer (D): Multiplying the number by 2 (whereas shifting right by 1 bit divides by 2) — Each 1-bit Left Shift multiplies by 2; each 1-bit Right Shift divides by 2.
Q6.3: How many Flip-Flops are required to construct a MOD-16 binary counter, and what is the maximum count it reaches?
(A) 4 Flip-Flops (`2⁴ = 16`) ✅
(B) 16 Flip-Flops
(C) 8 Flip-Flops
(D) 5 Flip-Flops
Answer (A): 4 Flip-Flops (`2⁴ = 16`); counts from 0 (0000) to 15 (1111). An `n`-bit binary counter has `2^n` states (`0` to `2^n − 1`).
Q6.4: What is the minimum number of Flip-Flops required to construct a Decade Counter (MOD-10 BCD Counter)?
(A) 10 Flip-Flops
(B) 4 Flip-Flops ✅
(C) 3 Flip-Flops
(D) 5 Flip-Flops
Answer (B): 4 Flip-Flops (since 2³ = 8 < 10 ≤ 2⁴ = 16) — A MOD-N counter requires `n = ⌈log₂(N)⌉` flip-flops; for MOD-10, `⌈log₂(10)⌉ = 4` flip-flops (with 6 skipped states).
Q6.5: If a MOD-10 counter is cascaded in series with a MOD-6 counter, what is the overall Modulus of the combined counter?
(A) MOD-16 (10 + 6)
(B) MOD-4
(C) MOD-60 (10 × 6 = 60) ✅
(D) MOD-100
Answer (C): Cascading a MOD-M and a MOD-N counter produces a `MOD-(M × N)` counter (dividing input frequency by `M × N`).

📌 7.1 Digital IC Logic Families (TTL, CMOS, ECL), Fan-Out & Figure of Merit
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Noise Margin: NM_H = V_OH(min) − V_IH(min), NM_L = V_IL(max) − V_OL(max)
  • 🔹 Figure of Merit (Speed-Power Product) = t_pd (ns) × P_D (mW) [in picoJoules, pJ]
Simple Exam Points
  • Comparison of IC Logic Families: (1) CMOS (Complementary MOSFET): Lowest static power dissipation (~nW), highest Fan-Out (>50), widest noise margin, and highest packing density (used in VLSI microprocessors/RAM)
  • (2) ECL (Emitter-Coupled Logic): Fastest switching speed (~0.5–1 ns propagation delay) because bipolar transistors operate in active region and NEVER enter saturation, at the cost of highest power dissipation
  • (3) TTL (Transistor-Transistor Logic, 74xx series): Uses multi-emitter BJT input and Totem-Pole output
  • unconnected (floating) TTL input acts as Logic HIGH (1).
  • Wired-AND is supported by Open-Collector TTL.
✅ Direct Exam Example: ADC & DAC Speed Ranking: Fastest Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC) is Flash / Parallel Comparator ADC (requires 2^n − 1 comparators for n-bit resolution, 1 clock cycle); Most accurate noise-rejecting ADC for digital multimeters is Dual-Slope Integrating ADC; Successive Approximation (SAR) ADC takes exactly n clock cycles for n bits.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: Sabse FASTEST logic family = ECL (Emitter-Coupled Logic — transistors never saturate!); aur sabse LOWEST Power Consumption & HIGHEST Fan-Out = CMOS! Floating TTL input hamesha Logic '1' ki tarah behave karta hai.

📝 Chapter 7 — Practice Questions & Simple Explanations

Q7.1: Which semiconductor memory is used to build ultra-fast CPU Cache memory (L1/L2/L3), and which is used for Main RAM?
(A) DRAM for Cache and SRAM for Main RAM
(B) EPROM for Cache
(C) SRAM for Cache; DRAM for Main RAM ✅
(D) Flash ROM for CPU Registers
Answer (C): SRAM (6-transistor flip-flop, no refresh) for Cache; DRAM (1-transistor + 1-capacitor, needs periodic refresh) for Main RAM — SRAM is faster and needs no refresh cycle (used in Cache); DRAM is denser and cheaper per bit but leaks charge and needs refreshing (used in Main Memory).
Q7.2: How is data erased in an EPROM (such as 2764) vs an EEPROM / Flash Memory?
(A) EPROM is erased electrically and EEPROM is erased by UV light
(B) Both can only be written once (like PROM)
(C) Both lose data when power is turned off
(D) EPROM is erased by exposing the quartz window to Ultraviolet (UV) light ✅
Answer (D): EPROM is erased by exposing the quartz window to Ultraviolet (UV) light; EEPROM / Flash is erased electrically. PROM = one-time fusible link; EPROM = UV-erasable (15–20 min); EEPROM = byte-level electrically erasable; Flash = block-level electrically erasable.
Q7.3: Among digital IC logic families (TTL, ECL, CMOS), which family has the LOWEST power consumption, and which is the FASTEST?
(A) CMOS has the lowest static power dissipation; ECL is the fastest ✅
(B) TTL has the lowest power and CMOS is the fastest
(C) ECL has the lowest power consumption
(D) TTL is faster than ECL
Answer (A): CMOS has the lowest static power dissipation; ECL (Emitter-Coupled Logic) is the fastest — CMOS consumes virtually zero static power and has the highest fan-out; ECL operates transistors out of saturation for the fastest switching speed.
Q7.4: What does 'Fan-Out' of a digital logic gate specify?
(A) The number of inputs on a single gate
(B) The maximum number of standard logic gate inputs that the output of a single gate can reliably drive ✅
(C) The heat dissipated by a cooling fan
(D) The propagation delay in nanoseconds
Answer (B): The maximum number of standard logic gate inputs (of the same family) that the output of a single gate can reliably drive — Fan-Out measures the current-driving capability of a gate's output without logic voltage degradation.
Q7.5: Which Digital-to-Analog Converter (DAC) uses only TWO resistor values (`R` and `2R`) regardless of the number of bits `n`?
(A) Binary-Weighted Resistor DAC (needs n different resistors: R, 2R, 4R, ..., 2^(n−1)R)
(B) Flash ADC
(C) R-2R Ladder DAC ✅
(D) Dual-Slope ADC
Answer (C): An R-2R Ladder DAC is easy to fabricate accurately in ICs because it uses only two resistor values (`R` and `2R`).
