📖 Chapter 1: Coding-Decoding: EJOTY Positional Encoding

14 Questions in Booklet
📌 1.1 EJOTY Positional Encoding & Forward/Backward Alphabet Shifts
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Forward Rank = Pos(L) [1..26]
  • 🔹 Backward/Reverse Rank = 27 − Pos(L)
  • 🔹 EJOTY = 5, 10, 15, 20, 25
Simple Exam Points
  • Every English alphabet letter has a forward position (A=1, B=2 ...
  • Z=26) and a reverse position from Z (Z=1, Y=2 ...
  • A=26) such that Forward + Reverse = 27.
  • Opposite letter pairs (sum = 27) are heavily tested in DSSSB coding: A-Z (Azad), B-Y (Boy), C-X (Crux), D-W (Dew), E-V (Evening), F-U (Fun), G-T (GT Road), H-S (High School), I-R (Indian Railway), J-Q (Jungle Queen), K-P (Kanpur), L-O (Love), M-N (Man).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: If 'DELHI' (4, 5, 12, 8, 9) is coded by replacing each letter with its opposite pair (27 − Pos), we get W(23), V(22), O(15), S(19), R(18) -> 'WVOSR'.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: Kisi bhi letter ki Reverse Position nikalne ke liye hamesha 27 me se uski Forward Position minus karein (Reverse = 27 − Forward). Jaise R = 18, to Reverse = 27 − 18 = 9 (I).
📌 1.2 Reverse-Order + Alternating Shift & Cross-Diagonal Letter Coding
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Reverse + Constant Shift: Code(W)[i] = Shift(W[n − 1 − i], +k)
  • 🔹 Cross-Pair Swap: (L1, L2) -> (L2+k, L1+k)
Simple Exam Points
  • In DSSSB TGT/PGT reasoning, 6-to-8 letter words are frequently split into two halves (first half reversed, second half reversed) or completely reversed before applying alternating shifts (+1, −1, +2, −2) or Vowel/Consonant conditional shifts (e.g., Vowels shifted by +2, Consonants shifted by −1).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: In 'COMPUTER' -> 'RFUVQNPC', reverse 'COMPUTER' to 'RETUPMOC'. Keep R and C at ends and shift middle letters by +1: R, E+1=F, T+1=U, U+1=V, P+1=Q, M+1=N, O+1=P, C -> 'RFUVQNPC'.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: Jab first letter aur coded word ka first letter bahut door dikhe, to turant input word ke LAST letter ko coded word ke FIRST letter se compare karein (Reverse Shift Pattern)!
📌 1.3 Positional Sum, Digital Root & Vowel-Consonant Weightage Coding
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Word Value = Σ Pos(L_i) × Count(L) or Σ (27 − Pos(L_i))
  • 🔹 Digital Root = 1 + ((N − 1) mod 9)
Simple Exam Points
  • Numerical coding assigns numbers to words using: (1) Direct sum of letter positions (e.g., CAT = 3+1+20 = 24), (2) Sum multiplied or divided by word length or number of vowels, (3) Sum of reverse positions, or (4) Reducing two-digit letter positions to a single digit by summing digits (e.g., R=18 -> 1+8=9).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: If GO = 32 and SHE = 49, find SOME. Using Reverse Positions: G(20)+O(12) = 32; S(8)+H(19)+E(22) = 49. Thus SOME = S(8)+O(12)+M(14)+E(22) = 56.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: Agar bada word chote number (jaise 5, 7, 8) me code ho raha hai, to Number of Letters, Number of Vowels × Consonants, ya Digital Root check karein!
📌 1.4 Chinese / Substitution Message Coding & Matrix Word Elimination
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Common Word Elimination across 3–4 statements: Intersect(Stmt_i, Stmt_j) -> Unique Code Token
Simple Exam Points
  • In multi-statement substitution coding (e.g., 'sky is blue' = '481', 'sea is deep' = '246'), compare pairs of sentences to isolate common words and their corresponding code tokens step by step.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: Given (1) 'pit na sa' = 'you are welcome', (2) 'na ho pa' = 'they are very good', (3) 'ka da na' = 'who are they'. Common to all three is 'are' = 'na'.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: 'What is called...' vs 'What is the code for...' me dhyan dein: 'If White is called Blue, Blue is called Red, Red is called Yellow, what is the colour of human blood?' -> Blood is Red, and Red is called Yellow -> Ans: Yellow!

📖 Chapter 2: Number Series & Analogies: Second-Order Difference, n² ± k

📌 2.1 Second-Order Difference, n² ± k & n³ ± n Number Series
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Cube-Minus-Self Series: a_n = n³ − n (0, 6, 24, 60, 120, 210, 336)
  • 🔹 Cube-Plus-Self: n³ + n (2, 10, 30, 68, 130, 222)
Simple Exam Points
  • When consecutive differences grow polynomially, compute first-order differences (Δ1) and second-order differences (Δ2).
  • Memorize signature cube/square landmarks: 60 = 4³−4, 68 = 4³+4, 120 = 5³−5 = 11²−1, 130 = 5³+5, 210 = 6³−6, 222 = 6³+6, 336 = 7³−7, 350 = 7³+7, 504 = 8³−8, 520 = 8³+8.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: Find the missing term in 7, 26, 63, 124, 215, 342, ? -> Pattern is n³ − 1 for n = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7. Next term for n = 8 is 8³ − 1 = 512 − 1 = 511.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: Series me 60, 120, 210, 336 dikhte hi turant n³ − n lagayein; aur 2, 10, 30, 68, 130, 222 dikhte hi n³ + n lagayein!
📌 2.2 Multiplicative-Cum-Additive & Half-Step Decimal Series (×0.5 + 0.5)
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Half-Step Pattern: T_2 = T_1 × 0.5 + 0.5; T_3 = T_2 × 1 + 1; T_4 = T_3 × 1.5 + 1.5; T_5 = T_4 × 2 + 2
Simple Exam Points
  • Whenever the second term of a series is approximately half of the first term and then the series starts rising again (e.g., 14, 8, 9, 14.5, 30), the multiplier starts at ×0.5 and increments by +0.5 at each step.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: In 14, 8, 9, 14.5, 30, ? -> 14×0.5+1=8; 8×1+1=9; 9×1.5+1=14.5; 14.5×2+1=30; Next = 30×2.5 + 1 = 76.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: Jab series pehle ghate aur phir tezi se badhe (jaise 14, 8, 9, 14.5, 30...), to 100% ×0.5 + 1, ×1 + 1, ×1.5 + 1, ×2 + 1 wala decimal multiplier lag raha hai!
📌 2.3 Number Triad Analogies & Missing Matrix Number Patterns
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Pythagorean Triads: (3,4,5), (5,12,13), (7,24,25), (8,15,17), (9,40,41), (11,60,61), (20,21,29)
Simple Exam Points
  • In Number Set/Triad questions (A, B, C), check in order: (1) A² + B² = C² or (A + B)×k = C, (2) |A² − B²| = C, (3) (A × B) / 2 = C, (4) B = A² ± k and C = A³ ± k.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: Given Triad (4, 17, 65): 17 = 4² + 1 and 65 = 4³ + 1 = (n, n²+1, n³+1). Analogous triad for n=5 is (5, 26, 126).
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: DSSSB ke latest instruction ke anusar Triad me numbers ko unke individual digits me todna mana hota hai — operations pure number par hi lagane hote hain!

📌 3.1 Family Tree Construction, Generation Levels & Pointing Statements
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Generation Levels: +2 (Grandparents)
  • 🔹 +1 (Parents/Uncles/Aunts/In-laws)
  • 🔹 0 (Self/Spouse/Siblings/Cousins)
  • 🔹 −1 (Children/Nephew/Niece)
Simple Exam Points
  • Use standard notation: [+] or Box for Male, [−] or Circle for Female, (<=>) for Married Couple, (—) for Siblings, and vertical line (|) for parent-child.
  • In pointing problems ('Pointing to a photograph, X said...'), always split the sentence at the word 'MY' and trace step-by-step from the speaker ('my' = speaker).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: 'Pointing to a man, Riya said: His mother is the only daughter of my mother.' 'Only daughter of Riya's mother' = Riya herself! So the man's mother is Riya -> Riya is the Mother of that man.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: 'A is the brother of B' me A ka gender Male (+) confirm hai, par B ka gender unknown hai (B भाई भी हो सकता है और बहन भी)! Name se kabhi gender assume na karein.
📌 3.2 Coded Blood Relations (A + B, A × B) & Backward Gender Elimination
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Coded Expression A @ B # C $ D: Evaluate Left-to-Right (Forward) or Right-to-Left (Backward) + Eliminate by Gender & Generation
Simple Exam Points
  • When asked 'Which of the following expressions means P is the Maternal Uncle of Q?', immediately check Two Filters before drawing any tree: (1) Gender Filter: P MUST be Male (+).
  • If P stands at the very end of a forward-coded expression (e.g., X + Y − P), P's gender is undetermined (unless the last relation is husband/wife), so eliminate that option instantly!
  • (2) Generation Filter: P must be +1 generation above Q.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: If A + B means A is sister of B, A − B means A is brother of B, A × B means A is mother of B. To show P is maternal uncle of R: P must be brother (−) of Q who is mother (×) of R -> Expression is 'P − Q × R'.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: Coded Blood Relation me 4 options draw karne me 2 minute waste mat karo — Gender Check aur Generation Gap (+1, 0, −1) se 3 options 10 second me eliminate ho jate hain!

📖 Chapter 4: Direction & Distance: Pythagorean Shortest Distance

11 Questions in Booklet
📌 4.1 Pythagorean Shortest Distance & Consecutive 90° Turn Cancellation
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Shortest Aerial Distance d = √(Δx² + Δy²)
  • 🔹 Net Turn Angle = Σ(Right 90° turns) − Σ(Left 90° turns)
Simple Exam Points
  • Track movements on a Cartesian coordinate plane starting at Origin (0,0): East = +x, West = −x, North = +y, South = −y.
  • For final facing direction questions without distance, simply cancel every Right turn (90° CW) with a Left turn (90° ACW).
  • Four identical turns (4R or 4L = 360°) return to the same direction
  • two identical turns (2R or 2L = 180°) reverse the direction.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: A person walks 10 m North, turns Right and walks 12 m East, then turns Right and walks 5 m South. Net North Δy = 10 − 5 = 5 m North; Net East Δx = 12 m East. Shortest distance from start = √(12² + 5²) = √169 = 13 m North-East.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: 'From where did he start?' vs 'In which direction is he from the starting point?' — hamesha 'FROM' wale point par '+' (compass cross) banakar teer (arrow) chalayein!
📌 4.2 Sunrise (Morning) & Sunset (Evening) Shadow Geometry
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Morning (Sunrise in East): All Shadows fall WEST
  • 🔹 Evening (Sunset in West): All Shadows fall EAST
  • 🔹 12:00 Noon: Zero shadow
Simple Exam Points
  • When two people A and B stand facing each other talking: (1) In the Morning (Sun in East, Shadow in West): Facing NORTH -> Shadow is on LEFT
  • Facing SOUTH -> Shadow is on RIGHT.
  • (2) In the Evening (Sun in West, Shadow in East): Facing NORTH -> Shadow is on RIGHT
  • Facing SOUTH -> Shadow is on LEFT.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: One morning after sunrise, Vikram and Shailesh were standing facing each other. Vikram's shadow fell exactly towards his left hand. Since in Morning Left = North, Vikram is facing North, and Shailesh is facing South.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: Morning Trick = 'N-L / S-R' (Morning me North dekhne wale ke LEFT me shadow banti hai, South dekhne wale ke RIGHT me). Evening me iska ulta ('N-R / S-L') hota hai!

📖 Chapter 5: Order, Ranking & Seating: Linear Row Ranking, Total Persons

11 Questions in Booklet
📌 5.1 Linear Row Ranking, Total Persons & Overlapping Case (Minimum Strength)
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Standard Row Total: T = (L + R) − 1
  • 🔹 Non-Overlapping Total: T_max = L + R + Mid
  • 🔹 Overlapping (Minimum) Total: T_min = L + R − Mid − 2
Simple Exam Points
  • When two persons A (rank L from left) and B (rank R from right) interchange their positions and A's new rank from left becomes L_new: (1) Total persons in row T = (L_new + R_old) − 1
  • (2) Number of persons between A and B = |L_new − L_old| − 1.
  • For Minimum Total (Overlapping case), the condition Mid <= min(L, R) − 2 must hold.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: In a row of students, Aman is 15th from Left and Rohit is 20th from Right. When they interchange positions, Aman becomes 24th from Left. Total students = Aman's New Left (24) + Rohit's Old Right (20) − 1 = 43 students. Students between them = 24 − 15 − 1 = 8.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: Position Interchange wale sawal me: Total = (Ek vyakti ki Nayi Position + Dusre vyakti ki Purani Position) − 1!
📌 5.2 Circular Seating Arrangement (Facing Center vs Facing Outside)
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Facing Center (Inward): Left = Clockwise (CW), Right = Anti-Clockwise (ACW)
  • 🔹 Facing Outside: Left = ACW, Right = CW
Simple Exam Points
  • In an even-numbered circular table (6 or 8 persons), 'diagonally opposite' means exactly N/2 seats away (for 8 persons, 4th to the left/right).
  • Note: 'A sits to the left of B, WHO sits 2nd to the right of C' -> 'WHO/WHICH/WHOSE' refers to the second person (B), whereas 'AND/BUT/WHILE' refers to the first person (A)!
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: 8 friends sit around a circular table facing the center. P is 3rd to the left of Q, who is 2nd to the right of R. If R is at seat 1, Q is at seat 3 (2nd right ACW), and P is 3rd left (CW) of Q -> seat 8.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: 'Who / Whom / Whose' hamesha comma ke turant pehle wale (2nd person) ke liye aata hai, jabki 'And / But / While' sentence ke 1st person ke liye aata hai!

📖 Chapter 6: Syllogisms — Classical Venn Syllogisms, Complementary Pair (Either-Or) & 'Only a Few'

📌 6.1 Classical Venn Syllogisms, Complementary Pair (Either-Or) & 'Only a Few'
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Complementary 'Either-Or' Rules: (1) Both conclusions individually false/doubtful, (2) Same subject-predicate, (3) Pair is (Some + No) or (All + Some Not)
Simple Exam Points
  • Modern DSSSB syllogisms test two special quantifiers: (1) 'Only a few A are B' = 'Some A are B' (definite true) AND 'Some A are NOT B' (definite true)!
  • Thus 'All A are B is a possibility' is FALSE, whereas 'All B are A is a possibility' is TRUE!
  • (2) 'Only A are B' = 'All B are A' AND 'B cannot intersect with anything other than A'!
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: Statements: Only a few Rivers are Lakes. All Lakes are Oceans. Conclusions: I. All Rivers being Oceans is a possibility (TRUE — Rivers is restricted only from being 100% inside Lakes, not Oceans). II. Some Rivers are not Lakes (TRUE — directly implied by 'Only a few').
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: 'All + No' kabhi bhi Complementary Either-Or pair NAHI banate (kyunki dono ek sath false ho sakte hain jab 'Some' true ho)! Valid Either-Or pairs sirf: (1) Some + No, aur (2) All + Some Not (same order) hain!
