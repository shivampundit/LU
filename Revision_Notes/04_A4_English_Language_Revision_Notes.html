📖 Chapter 1: Spotting Errors & SVA: Subject-Verb Agreement — Rule of First Subject vs Rule of Proximity

13 Questions in Booklet
📌 1.1 Subject-Verb Agreement — Rule of First Subject vs Rule of Proximity
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Rule 1 (1st Subject): as well as, along with, together with, in addition to, accompanied by
  • 🔹 Rule 2 (Closer Subject): neither..nor, either..or, not only..but also
Simple Exam Points
  • When two subjects S1 and S2 are joined by prepositional connectors ('as well as', 'along with', 'with', 'together with', 'besides', 'and not'), the verb strictly agrees with the FIRST subject S1.
  • When joined by correlative conjunctions ('neither...nor', 'either...or', 'not only...but also'), the verb agrees with the NEARER subject S2.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: 'The Captain, along with his eleven players, WAS felicitated' (agrees with singular 'The Captain'); whereas 'Neither the Captain nor the players WERE felicitated' (agrees with plural 'players').
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: 'Each, Every, Either, Neither, Everyone, Somebody, Nobody, Many a + Singular Noun' ke sath hamesha SINGULAR Verb aata hai! ('Many a soldier HAS died').
📌 1.2 One of + Plural Noun + Relative Clause (Who/Which/That) & Collective Nouns
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 One of the + Plural Noun + Singular Verb
  • 🔹 One of the + Plural Noun + who/that + PLURAL Verb (unless preceded by 'The only one of')
Simple Exam Points
  • In 'One of my friends IS an engineer', the main verb is singular ('is').
  • However, in a relative clause 'Rohit is one of the students who HAVE passed the exam', the antecedent of 'who' is the plural noun 'students', so the verb after 'who' MUST be plural ('have')!
  • Exception: If 'the only' precedes 'one' ('Rohit is the ONLY one of the students who HAS passed'), the verb becomes singular ('has').
✅ Direct Exam Example: Uncountable Singular Nouns (never take -s or 'a/an'): Scenery, Poetry, Furniture, Advice, Information, Hair, Luggage, Baggage, Bread, Machinery, Equipment, Evidence, Stationery -> Always Singular Verb!
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: 'A number of + Plural Noun' takes a PLURAL Verb ('A number of students ARE absent'), whereas 'The number of + Plural Noun' takes a SINGULAR Verb ('The number of students IS fifty')!

📖 Chapter 2: Tenses & Conditionals: Four Conditional Clause Structures (Zero, First, Second, Third

9 Questions in Booklet
📌 2.1 Four Conditional Clause Structures (Zero, First, Second, Third & Inverted Had)
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Type 1: If + V1/s/es -> will + V1
  • 🔹 Type 2: If + V2/were -> would + V1
  • 🔹 Type 3: If + had + V3 -> would have + V3 (Inverted: Had + S + V3, S + would have + V3)
Simple Exam Points
  • Never use 'will/would' inside the 'If / Unless / Until / When / As soon as / Provided' conditional clause itself!
  • Also master the Past Subjunctive after 'It is time / It is high time / It is about time + Subject + V2 (Simple Past)' (e.g., 'It is high time you STARTED studying').
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: Error in 'If you would have informed me earlier, I would have helped you' -> Replace 'If you would have informed' with 'If you had informed' (or 'Had you informed').
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: 'If I were...' (Imaginary / Unreal status) me har subject (I/He/She/It) ke sath 'WERE' aata hai, aur main clause me 'would + V1' aata hai! Aur 'Had + V3' ke sath hamesha 'would have + V3' aata hai.
📌 2.2 Present Perfect ('Since' vs 'For') vs Simple Past & Past Perfect ('Before/After')
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Past Action 1 (Earlier) = Past Perfect (had + V3) + 'before' + Past Action 2 (Later) = Simple Past (V2)
Simple Exam Points
  • Use 'Since + Point of Time' (since 2018, since Monday, since childhood) and 'For + Period/Duration of Time' (for 5 years, for two hours) with Present/Past Perfect Continuous.
  • Note: When 'Since' joins two clauses, the structure is: Present Perfect (has/have + V3) + SINCE + Simple Past (V2) (e.g., 'Ten years HAVE passed since I MET him').
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: 'The patient HAD DIED (Past Perfect, 1st action) before the doctor ARRIVED (Simple Past, 2nd action).'
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: Definite past time markers ('yesterday, ago, last night, in 2015') ke sath kabhi bhi Present Perfect (has/have + V3) nahi lagta — hamesha Simple Past (V2) lagta hai!

📌 3.1 Fixed Prepositions, Latin Comparatives ('To' vs 'Than') & Phrasal Verbs
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Latin Adjectives taking 'TO' (not 'than'): Senior, Junior, Superior, Inferior, Prior, Anterior, Posterior, Prefer / Preferable
Simple Exam Points
  • Transitive verbs that take NO preposition in active voice: 'discuss, describe, order, request, investigate, comprise, enter (a room), resemble, attack, emphasize, await' (Note: 'consist of' = 'comprise'
  • 'wait for' = 'await'
  • 'look into' = 'investigate').
  • Key Fixed Pairs: Accused OF, Acquitted OF, Guilty OF, Abstain FROM, Prevent FROM, Refrain FROM, Congratulate ON, Insist ON, Fond OF, Abide BY, Comply WITH, Cope WITH (never 'cope up with'!).
✅ Direct Exam Example: High-Yield Phrasal Verbs: Call off (Cancel), Put off (Postpone), Put out (Extinguish fire), Look after (Take care of), Look down upon (Despise), Give in (Surrender/Yield), Give up (Abandon habit), Bring up (Rear/Educate a child), Turn down (Reject), Break out (Spread suddenly — war/epidemic), Make out (Understand/Decipher).
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: 'Cope up with' galat English hai — sahi phrase 'Cope with' (manage successfully) hota hai! Aur 'Despite' ke sath kabhi 'of' nahi lagta (Despite = In spite of).

📖 Chapter 4: Voice & Narration — Active-Passive Voice Transformation & Direct-Indirect Narration Rules

31 Questions in Booklet
📌 4.1 Active-Passive Voice Transformation & Direct-Indirect Narration Rules
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Passive Core: Be-form (is/am/are/was/were/been/being) + V3 (Past Participle)
  • 🔹 'Who' in Active -> 'By whom + Aux + Subj + V3?' in Passive
Simple Exam Points
  • In Active-to-Passive Voice, tense NEVER changes (Present Continuous 'is writing' -> 'is being written'
  • Past Simple 'wrote' -> 'was written'
  • Modals 'must finish' -> 'must be finished').
  • Verbs taking non-'by' prepositions in Passive: 'known TO me', 'surprised/astonished AT his behaviour', 'satisfied/pleased WITH your work', 'contained/interested IN this box'.
  • In Direct-to-Indirect Speech, when the Reporting Verb is in the Past ('said'), tense shifts back one step (V1 -> V2, V2 -> had+V3, will -> would, can -> could) UNLESS the reported speech is a Universal Truth or Proverb!
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: Active: 'Who taught you Computer Science?' -> Passive: 'By whom were you taught Computer Science?' (or 'Who were you taught Computer Science by?').
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: Voice (Active-Passive) me Tense kabhi change NAHI hota, jabki Narration (Direct-Indirect) me Past Reporting Verb hone par Tense change HOTA hai (aur Interrogative Indirect me helping verb subject ke BAAD aata hai: 'asked where I WAS going', NOT 'where was I going')!

📌 7.1 Para-Jumbles (PQRS Sentence Rearrangement), Cloze Test & Reading Comprehension
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Mandatory Pair Anchors: Full Name -> Pronoun/Surname
  • 🔹 Indefinite Noun ('A king') -> Definite ('The king')
  • 🔹 Cause -> 'Therefore/Hence' Effect
Simple Exam Points
  • To solve PQRS Para-Jumbles in under 20 seconds: (1) Eliminate starter sentences beginning with pronouns (He, She, They, It, This, These) or contrastive conjunctions (However, But, Nevertheless, Moreover, Therefore)
  • (2) Identify the independent Noun/Topic sentence
  • (3) Lock at least ONE Mandatory Pair (e.g., Chronological sequence 1991 -> 2005, or Question -> Answer, or 'Not only' -> 'But also') and eliminate options lacking that pair.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: If sentence R introduces 'Dr. Vikram Sarabhai established INCOSPAR in 1962' and sentence P says 'He later spearheaded the formation of ISRO in 1969', then 'RP' forms a locked chronological & noun-pronoun pair.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: Reading Comprehension me 'Inference / Tone' wale question me kabhi bhi extreme options ('always, never, completely') chunne se bachein — passage ke scope ke andar hi answer chunein!

  • 💡 [Shortcut] Exam Trap: Definite past time markers ('yesterday, ago, last night, in 2015') ke sath kabhi bhi Present Perfect (has/have + V3) nahi lagta — hamesha Simple Past (V2) lagta hai!

⚡ Chapter 4 One-Liners — Voice & Narration — Active-Passive Voice Transformation & Direct-Indirect Narration Rules

  • 🔹 [Active-Passive Voice Transformation] Passive Core: Be-form (is/am/are/was/were/been/being) + V3 (Past Participle)
  • 🔹 [Active-Passive Voice Transformation] 'Who' in Active -> 'By whom + Aux + Subj + V3?' in Passive
  • 💡 [Shortcut] Exam Trap: Voice (Active-Passive) me Tense kabhi change NAHI hota, jabki Narration (Direct-Indirect) me Past Reporting Verb hone par Tense change HOTA hai (aur Interrogative Indirect me helping verb subject ke BAAD aata hai: 'asked where I WAS going', NOT 'where was I going')!
