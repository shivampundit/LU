📌 1.1 Electrostatics — Coulomb's Law, Gauss's Law, Electric Potential & Parallel-Plate Capacitor Networks
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Coulomb's Law: F = (1 / 4πε_0) · |q1·q2| / r²
  • 🔹 Gauss's Law: ∮ E · dA = Q_enclosed / ε_0
  • 🔹 Capacitor C = Q / V = K·ε_0·A / d
  • 🔹 Stored Energy U = ½ C V² = Q² / (2C) = ½ Q V
Simple Exam Points
  • Inside a charged hollow metallic conductor in electrostatic equilibrium, the **Electric Field `E` is ZERO (`E = 0`)**, while the **Electric Potential `V` is CONSTANT and NON-ZERO** throughout the interior (equal to the surface potential — the principle behind **Faraday Cage** electromagnetic shielding!).
  • Capacitance `C = K ε_0 A / d`: Inserting a dielectric slab of dielectric constant `K > 1` increases capacitance `K` times (`C' = K·C_0`).
  • Capacitor Combinations (**opposite to resistors!**): (1) **In Parallel**: `C_eq = C1 + C2 + ...
  • + Cn` (same voltage `V` across each)
  • (2) **In Series**: `1/C_eq = 1/C1 + 1/C2 + ...` (same charge `Q` on each).
  • Watch the battery disconnect trap when inserting a dielectric `K`: If Battery remains **Connected** (`V` constant): `C -> KC, Q -> KQ, E -> E, U -> KU`
  • if Battery is **Disconnected** (`Q` constant): `C -> KC, V -> V/K, E -> E/K, U -> U/K`!
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Capacitor Math: Three capacitors of `6 μF` each are connected in Series across a `12 V` battery. `1/C_eq = 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 3/6 => C_eq = 2 μF`. Total stored electrostatic energy `U = ½ C_eq V² = 0.5 × 2 μF × 12² = 144 μJ`.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: Capacitors ka combination Resistors ka exact ULTA hota hai: **Parallel me `C_eq = C1 + C2`** (judte hain) aur **Series me `1/C_eq = 1/C1 + 1/C2`**! Aur khokhle dhatu ke gole (Hollow Conductor) ke andar Electric Field `E = 0` hota hai par Potential `V = Constant` rehta hai!

Q1.1: What are the SI units of (1) Electric Charge, (2) Electric Potential (Voltage), (3) Capacitance, (4) Inductance, (5) Magnetic Flux, and (6) Magnetic Flux Density (Magnetic Field B)?
(A) Charge = Coulomb (C) ✅
(B) Magnetic Field B is measured in Farads
(C) Inductance is measured in Tesla
(D) 1 Tesla = 10⁻⁴ Gauss
Answer (A): Charge = Coulomb (C); Voltage = Volt (V = J/C); Capacitance = Farad (F = C/V); Inductance = Henry (H); Magnetic Flux = Weber (Wb); Magnetic Field B = Tesla (T = Wb/m² = 10⁴ Gauss) — Memorize: `1 Tesla = 1 Weber / m² = 10⁴ Gauss`. And Electric Power `P = V × I = I² R = V² / R` Watts.
Q1.2: Inside a hollow charged conducting spherical shell, what is the Electric Field `E` and Electric Potential `V`?
(A) Both E and V are zero inside
(B) Electric Field E = 0 everywhere inside ✅
(C) E is infinite and V is zero
(D) E is constant and non-zero
Answer (B): Electric Field E = 0 everywhere inside; Electric Potential V is CONSTANT inside (equal to the potential on the surface) — This is the principle of **Electrostatic Shielding** (Faraday Cage — why a car is safe during lightning).

📌 2.1 Current Electricity — Ohm's Law, Temperature Coefficient, Kirchhoff's Laws (KCL/KVL), Wheatstone Bridge & Max Power
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Resistance R = ρ·L / A
  • 🔹 Stretching a wire to n times length (Volume constant): R_new = n² · R_old
  • 🔹 KCL (Junction Law) = Conservation of Charge (Σ I_in = Σ I_out)
  • 🔹 KVL (Loop Law) = Conservation of Energy (Σ ΔV = 0)
Simple Exam Points
  • Resistivity `ρ` depends ONLY on the material and temperature (NEVER on wire length `L` or cross-sectional area `A`!).
  • However, if a wire of resistance `R` is **stretched** so its length becomes `n·L`, its cross-sectional area shrinks to `A/n` (constant volume `V = A·L`), so its new resistance increases by **`n²`** (`R' = n²·R`)!
  • Temperature Coefficient of Resistance (`α`): Metals/Conductors have **Positive `α`** (Resistance rises with temperature)
  • Semiconductors (Si, Ge) and Insulators have **Negative `α` (NTC)** (Resistance drops exponentially as temperature rises due to thermal carrier generation!).
  • **Wheatstone Bridge Balance Condition**: `P / Q = R / S` (zero current through central galvanometer).
  • **Maximum Power Transfer Theorem**: A DC source with internal resistance `r` delivers maximum power to load `R_L` when **`R_L = r`** (at which efficiency is 50% and `P_max = E² / (4r)`).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Wire Stretch & KCL Math: A copper wire of resistance `10 Ω` is stretched uniformly to double its original length (`n = 2`). New resistance `R_new = 2² × 10 = 40 Ω`. If this `40 Ω` wire is bent into a closed circle and resistance is measured across any diameter (two `20 Ω` halves in parallel), `R_diam = (20 × 20)/(20 + 20) = 10 Ω`.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: **Kirchhoff's 1st Law (KCL / Node Law)** **Conservation of Electric Charge** par aadharit hai, jabki **Kirchhoff's 2nd Law (KVL / Loop Law)** **Conservation of Energy** par aadharit hai! Aur taar ko खींचकर (stretch karke) `n` guna lamba karne par Resistance `n` guna nahi balki **`n²` guna** ho jata hai!

Q2.1: Three resistors of `3 Ω, 6 Ω, and 2 Ω` are connected in PARALLEL across a `12 V` DC battery. What is the equivalent resistance `R_eq` and the total current `I` drawn from the battery?
(A) R_eq = 11 Ω; I = 1.09 A
(B) R_eq = 2 Ω; I = 6 A
(C) R_eq = 1 Ω; Total current I = V / R_eq = 12 V / 1 Ω = 12 A ✅
(D) R_eq = 0.5 Ω; I = 24 A
Answer (C): R_eq = 1 Ω (since 1/R_eq = 1/3 + 1/6 + 1/2 = 6/6 = 1); Total current I = V / R_eq = 12 V / 1 Ω = 12 A — For parallel resistors: `1/R_eq = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃`; for parallel capacitors: `C_eq = C₁ + C₂ + C₃` (opposite of resistors!).
Q2.2: According to Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL — Node Rule) and Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL — Loop Rule), which fundamental physical conservation laws are they based on?
(A) KCL is Conservation of Energy and KVL is Conservation of Charge
(B) Both are based on Conservation of Mass
(C) KVL applies only to open circuits
(D) KCL is based on Conservation of Electric Charge; KVL is based on Conservation of Energy ✅
Answer (D): KCL (`Σ I = 0` at a junction) is based on Conservation of Electric Charge; KVL (`Σ V = 0` around a closed loop) is based on Conservation of Energy — And **Lenz's Law** (`ε = −N dΦ_B / dt`, the minus sign in Faraday's Law) is ALSO a direct consequence of the **Law of Conservation of Energy**!
Q2.3: According to the Barkhausen Criterion for sustained sinusoidal oscillations in a feedback oscillator circuit (with amplifier gain `A` and feedback factor `β`), what two conditions must the loop gain `A · β` satisfy?
(A) 1. Magnitude of loop gain |A · β| = 1 (Unity), and 2. Total Phase Shift around the closed loop = 0° ✅
(B) Loop gain |A · β| = 0 and Phase Shift = 180° (Negative Feedback)
(C) |A · β| < 0.5
(D) Oscillators use Negative Feedback
Answer (A): 1. Magnitude of loop gain |A · β| = 1 (Unity), and 2. Total Phase Shift around the closed loop = 0° (or an integer multiple of 360°, i.e., Positive Regenerative Feedback) — Amplifiers use **Negative Feedback** (`180°` out of phase, stabilizing gain and increasing bandwidth), whereas Oscillators (RC Phase-Shift, Wien-Bridge, Hartley, Colpitts, Crystal) use **Positive Feedback** (`0° / 360°` phase shift, `|Aβ| ≥ 1`).
Q2.4: According to Coulomb's Law, if the distance `r` between two point charges `q₁` and `q₂` is DOUBLED (`2r`), what happens to the electrostatic force `F` between them?
(A) It becomes half (`F / 2`)
(B) It becomes ONE-FOURTH ✅
(C) It doubles (`2F`)
(D) It remains unchanged
Answer (B): It becomes ONE-FOURTH (`F / 4`, inverse-square law `F ∝ 1/r²`) — `F = k · q₁q₂ / r²`: doubling `r` reduces `F` by a factor of `2² = 4`.
Q2.5: If two capacitors of `6 μF` and `3 μF` are connected in (1) SERIES and (2) PARALLEL, what are their equivalent capacitances?
(A) Series = 9 μF; Parallel = 2 μF
(B) Both are 4.5 μF
(C) Series: (6 × 3) / (6 + 3) = 2 μF; Parallel: 6 + 3 = 9 μF ✅
(D) Series = 18 μF
Answer (C): Capacitors combine **opposite** to resistors: Parallel adds directly (`C₁ + C₂`), Series adds reciprocals (`1/C₁ + 1/C₂`).

📌 3.1 Semiconductor Physics — Intrinsic/Extrinsic (n-Type vs p-Type), Mass-Action Law, PN Junction & Zener/Opto Diodes
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Mass-Action Law (Thermal Equilibrium): n · p = n_i²
  • 🔹 Barrier Potential V_0 at 300K: Silicon (Si, E_g = 1.12 eV) ≈ 0.7 V; Germanium (Ge, E_g = 0.72 eV) ≈ 0.3 V
  • 🔹 Half-Wave Rectifier Ripple Freq = f_in (η = 40.6%)
  • 🔹 Full-Wave = 2·f_in (η = 81.2%)
Simple Exam Points
  • **Intrinsic Semiconductor** (pure Si/Ge, Group 14 tetravalent: `n = p = n_i`).
  • **Extrinsic Doping**: (1) **n-Type**: Doped with Pentavalent Group 15 **Donor** impurities (**Phosphorus P, Arsenic As, Antimony Sb**)
  • electrons are majority carriers (`n >> p`), Fermi level shifts toward Conduction Band
  • (2) **p-Type**: Doped with Trivalent Group 13 **Acceptor** impurities (**Boron B, Aluminium Al, Gallium Ga, Indium In**)
  • holes are majority carriers (`p >> n`), Fermi level shifts toward Valence Band.
  • Crucial Rule: **Both n-type and p-type semiconductors are electrically NEUTRAL overall!** Special Diodes: (1) **Zener Diode**: Heavily doped PN junction operated in **Reverse Breakdown Region** as a **Constant-Voltage Regulator (Shunt Regulator)**
  • (2) **Photodiode & Solar Cell**: Photodiode operates in **Reverse Bias** converting light into current
  • (3) **LED**: Forward-biased direct-bandgap compound semiconductor (GaAs, GaP, GaN)
  • (4) **Varactor (Varicap) Diode**: Reverse-biased voltage-controlled variable capacitor used in FM/TV tuners
  • (5) **Tunnel (Esaki) Diode**: Degenerately doped PN junction exhibiting **Negative Differential Resistance** due to quantum mechanical tunneling.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Mass-Action Law Numerical: An intrinsic silicon wafer has `n_i = 1.5 × 10^10 cm^-3` at 300 K. It is doped with donor Phosphorus atoms of concentration `N_D = 4.5 × 10^15 cm^-3`. Majority electron concentration `n ≈ N_D = 4.5 × 10^15 cm^-3`, and minority hole concentration `p = n_i² / n = (2.25 × 10^20) / (4.5 × 10^15) = 5 × 10^4 cm^-3`.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: **n-Type** aur **p-Type** semiconductor dono ka net electric charge **ZERO (Electrically Neutral)** hota hai! **Zener Diode** aur **Photodiode** hamesha **Reverse Bias** me kaam karte hain, jabki **LED** **Forward Bias** me kaam karta hai!

At T = 0 K, an intrinsic semiconductor behaves as a PERFECT INSULATOR (valence band full, conduction band empty); as temperature increases, its resistance DECREASES (Negative Temperature Coefficient of Resistance, unlike metals!) — Metals have a **Positive** temperature coefficient (`R` increases with `T`), whereas Semiconductors and Insulators have a **Negative** temperature coefficient (`R` decreases exponentially as `T` rises).

(A) BOTH N-type and P-type semiconductor crystals are electrically NEUTRAL! ✅
(B) N-type is negatively charged and P-type is positively charged
(C) N-type is positively charged and P-type is negatively charged

Q3.3: When a PN Junction Diode is Forward Biased vs Reverse Biased, what happens to the width of the Depletion Region and the potential barrier?

Forward Bias: Depletion width DECREASES and barrier lowers (allowing diffusion current of majority carriers); Reverse Bias: Depletion width INCREASES and barrier rises (only tiny minority-carrier drift saturation current `I₀` flows) — In a Half-Wave Rectifier: max efficiency = `40.6%`, ripple factor = `1.21`, output ripple frequency = `f_in`; in a Full-Wave (Center-Tap or Bridge) Rectifier: max efficiency = `81.2%`, ripple factor = `0.482`, output ripple frequency = `2 f_in` (Bridge Rectifier needs 4 diodes and has PIV = `V_m`).

📖 Chapter 4: BJT & MOSFET Transistors — BJT Operating Regions (Active, Cutoff, Saturation), α-β Relation, MOSFET CMOS Inverter & Op-Amp Ideal Specs

4 Questions in Booklet
📌 4.1 BJT Operating Regions (Active, Cutoff, Saturation), α-β Relation, MOSFET CMOS Inverter & Op-Amp Ideal Specs
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 BJT Currents: I_E = I_B + I_C
  • 🔹 DC Current Gains: α = I_C / I_E (< 1, ~0.98–0.99); β = I_C / I_B (50–300)
  • 🔹 Conversion: β = α / (1 − α) and α = β / (β + 1)
Simple Exam Points
  • A **BJT (Bipolar Junction Transistor)** is a **Current-Controlled Current Device** (both electrons and holes participate) with 3 doped regions: **Emitter** (Heavily doped, moderate size), **Base** (Lightly doped and extremely thin), and **Collector** (Moderately doped, LARGEST physical area to dissipate heat!).
  • BJT Operating Modes by Junction Bias: (1) **Active Region (EB Forward, CB Reverse)** -> Used as a **Linear Amplifier**
  • (2) **Cutoff Region (EB Reverse, CB Reverse)** -> Digital Switch **OFF (Open Switch)**
  • (3) **Saturation Region (EB Forward, CB Forward)** -> Digital Switch **ON (Closed Switch)**.
  • Note: **Common-Emitter (CE)** configuration provides the highest Power Gain and **180° phase inversion** between input and output!
  • A **MOSFET** is a **Unipolar Voltage-Controlled Device** with near-infinite input impedance (`I_G ≈ 0`) due to the insulating SiO2 gate oxide layer.
  • A **CMOS Inverter** pairs a pull-up **PMOS** (conducts when Gate = 0) and a pull-down **NMOS** (conducts when Gate = 1), achieving near-zero static power dissipation!
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked BJT Gain Numerical: A transistor has common-base current gain `α = 0.98` and base current `I_B = 40 μA`. Find `β`, `I_C`, and `I_E`. First, `β = 0.98 / (1 − 0.98) = 0.98 / 0.02 = 49`. Collector current `I_C = β · I_B = 49 × 40 μA = 1.96 mA`. Emitter current `I_E = I_B + I_C = 0.04 + 1.96 = 2.00 mA`.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: BJT ko **Amplifier** ki tarah chalane ke liye **Active Region (EB=Forward, CB=Reverse)** me rakhte hain, aur **Digital Logic Switch** ki tarah chalane ke liye **Cutoff (OFF) aur Saturation (ON)** ke beech toggle karte hain! Aur `β = α / (1 − α)` formula har saal pucha jata hai!

📝 Chapter 4 — Practice Questions & Simple Explanations

Q4.1: Among the three BJT amplifier configurations — Common Base (CB), Common Emitter (CE), and Common Collector (CC / Emitter Follower) — which one provides a `180°` phase inversion between input and output voltage, and which one is used as an Impedance-Matching Buffer (`A_v ≈ 1`, High `Z_in`, Low `Z_out`)?
(A) Common Base provides 180° phase shift
(B) Common Emitter (CE) provides 180° phase shift and highest power gain ✅
(C) Common Emitter has 0° phase shift
(D) Common Collector has voltage gain of 1000
Answer (B): Common Emitter (CE) provides 180° phase shift and highest power gain; Common Collector has high Z_in, low Z_out, and unity voltage gain for impedance matching. Common Emitter (CE) provides 180° phase shift and highest power gain; Common Collector (CC / Emitter Follower) has high Z_in, low Z_out, and unity voltage gain (0° phase shift) for impedance matching — CE is the most widely used general-purpose amplifier (high current gain `β`, high voltage gain, highest power gain, and `180°` phase reversal).
Q4.2: In a BJT (NPN or PNP transistor), which of the three doped regions — Emitter, Base, or Collector — is (1) HEAVILY doped, (2) LIGHTLY doped & very thin, and (3) PHYSICALLY LARGEST in area?
(A) Base is heavily doped and thickest
(B) Emitter is lightly doped
(C) (1) Emitter = Heavily doped ✅
(D) Collector is the smallest region
Answer (C): (1) Emitter = Heavily doped; (2) Base = Very thin & lightly doped; (3) Collector = Moderately doped & physically LARGEST. (1) Emitter = Heavily doped (injects carriers); (2) Base = Very thin & lightly doped (minimizes recombination); (3) Collector = Moderately doped & physically LARGEST (dissipates heat) — Emitter injects carriers, thin Base passes ~98–99% of them through, and large Collector collects them and dissipates power.
Q4.3: In a Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT), what are thebiasing conditions of the Emitter-Base (EB) junction and Collector-Base (CB) junction for the three operating regions: (1) Active Region (Amplifier), (2) Saturation Region (Closed Switch ON), and (3) Cut-off Region (Open Switch OFF)?
(A) Active: Both Forward; Saturation: EB Forward, CB Reverse
(B) Active: Both Reverse; Cut-off: Both Forward
(C) Active: EB Reverse, CB Forward (Inverse Active)
(D) Active: EB Forward, CB Reverse; Saturation (ON): Both EB and CB Forward; Cut-off (OFF): Both EB and CB Reverse ✅
Answer (D): BJT operating regions: Active (EB=Forward, CB=Reverse -> linear amplification), Saturation (EB=Forward, CB=Forward -> closed switch ON), Cut-off (EB=Reverse, CB=Reverse -> open switch OFF).
Q4.4: In a BJT, if the Common-Base DC current gain is `α = I_C / I_E = 0.98`, what is the Common-Emitter DC current gain `β = I_C / I_B`?
(A) β = α / (1 − α) = 0.98 / (1 − 0.98) = 0.98 / 0.02 = 49 ✅
(B) β = 98
(C) β = 0.49
(D) β = 50
Answer (A): Since `I_E = I_B + I_C`, dividing by `I_C` gives `1/α = 1/β + 1`, so `β = α / (1 - α) = 0.98 / 0.02 = 49` (and conversely `α = β / (β + 1) = 49 / 50 = 0.98`).

📌 5.1 Optical Fiber Physics — Total Internal Reflection, Numerical Aperture (NA), Acceptance Angle, Attenuation & Laser
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Critical Angle: sin(θ_c) = n_2 / n_1 (Core n_1 > Cladding n_2)
  • 🔹 Numerical Aperture NA = sin(θ_a) = √(n_1² − n_2²) ≈ n_1 · √(2Δ) [in air n_0 = 1]
  • 🔹 Attenuation dB = 10 · log10(P_in / P_out)
Simple Exam Points
  • Optical Fiber waveguides transmit light pulses via **Total Internal Reflection (TIR)** at the Core-Cladding interface, requiring Core refractive index `n_1` to be strictly greater than Cladding refractive index `n_2` (`n_1 > n_2`, fractional index difference `Δ = (n_1 − n_2)/n_1 ≈ 1%`).
  • The maximum external angle with the fiber axis at which light can enter the core and still undergo TIR inside is the **Acceptance Angle `θ_a = arcsin(NA)`**, where **Numerical Aperture `NA = √(n_1² − n_2²)`** measures light-gathering capacity.
  • Fiber Modes & Dispersion: **Step-Index Single-Mode Fiber (SMF)** has a tiny 8–10 μm core (`V-number < 2.405`) supporting only 1 fundamental propagation path, completely eliminating **Modal (Intermodal) Dispersion** for long-haul transoceanic links

Simple Exam Points
  • Why Modulate?
  • Translating a low-frequency baseband signal onto a high-frequency carrier reduces required antenna height (`L ≈ λ/4 = c / (4f)`), prevents co-channel interference via frequency multiplexing, and extends wireless range.
  • **Amplitude Modulation (AM)**: `BW = 2f_m`
  • at 100% modulation (`μ = 1`), `P_t = 1.5 P_c` (meaning 2/3rd = 66.7% power is wasted in the non-information carrier and only 1/3rd = 33.3% is in the two sidebands -> solved by DSB-SC and SSB-SC which needs only `f_m` bandwidth!).

  • Sampling (at f_s >= 2·f_max per Nyquist) -> 2.
  • Quantization (rounding to L = 2^n discrete levels
  • introduces Quantization Noise with SQNR ≈ 6.02n + 1.76 dB!) -> 3.
  • Binary Encoding`**.
  • Digital Carrier Modulation: **ASK** (Amplitude), **FSK** (Frequency), **BPSK** (1 bit/symbol, 2 phases 0°/180°), **QPSK** (2 bits/symbol, 4 phases), **16-QAM** (4 bits/symbol, combines Amplitude + Phase), **64-QAM** (6 bits/symbol), **256-QAM** (8 bits/symbol).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Modulation & PCM Numerical: (1) A voice channel band-limited to `f_max = 4 kHz` is sampled at Nyquist rate (`f_s = 2 × 4 = 8,000 samples/sec`) and quantized into `256 levels` (`n = log2(256) = 8 bits/sample`). PCM Bit Rate = `8,000 × 8 = 64 kbps` (standard DS0 telephone channel!). (2) A 16-QAM modem operating at `2400 Baud` achieves Bit Rate = `2400 × log2(16) = 2400 × 4 = 9600 bps`.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: **PCM (Pulse Code Modulation)** ke 3 steps ka exact order: **Sampling -> Quantization -> Encoding ('S-Q-E')**! Aur **64-QAM** me har symbol (1 Baud) **`log2(64) = 6 bits`** carry karta hai (Bit Rate = 6 × Baud Rate)!

Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 CDMA Chip Coding: Orthogonal Walsh Codes (Dot Product C_i · C_j = 0 for i ≠ j, and C_i · C_i = N)
  • 🔹 Cellular Cluster Size N = i² + i·j + j² (Valid N = 1, 3, 4, 7, 9, 12, 13...)

Simple Exam Points
  • Multiplexing Techniques: **FDM** (Frequency Division — analog radio/TV, separated by guard bands), **WDM / DWDM** (Wavelength Division — optical fiber prisms multiplexing multiple light colors/λ), **TDM** (Time Division — Synchronous vs Statistical/Asynchronous TDM), and **CDMA (Code Division Multiple Access)**: All stations transmit simultaneously over the ENTIRE frequency band using Spread Spectrum and orthogonal bipolar `+1/−1` **Walsh Chip Sequences**!
  • Cellular Architecture uses **Hexagonal Cells** (maximizes coverage area per base station without overlap gaps)
  • valid frequency reuse cluster sizes follow `N = i² + ij + j²` (most commonly `N = 7`) with Frequency Reuse Factor `1/N`.
  • Satellite Orbits: **GEO (Geostationary Earth Orbit)** at `35,786 km` equatorial altitude (24-hr orbital period matching Earth's rotation, appears stationary to ground dish, 3 satellites cover the globe, ~250 ms one-way propagation latency)
  • **MEO** (`2,000–20,000 km` — GPS/GLONASS/Galileo/NavIC navigation constellations at ~20,200 km with 12-hr period)
  • **LEO (Low Earth Orbit)** (`160–2,000 km` — Starlink, Iridium, ISS
  • ultra-low latency ~25 ms).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked CDMA Decoding Math: Station A has chip code `C_A = [+1, −1, +1, −1]` and Station B has orthogonal code `C_B = [+1, +1, −1, −1]`. Station A sends bit `1` (`+1` -> `[+1, −1, +1, −1]`) and Station B sends bit `0` (`−1` -> `[−1, −1, +1, +1]`). Combined channel signal `S = C_A − C_B = [0, −2, +2, 0]`. To recover Station A's bit, Receiver computes inner product `S · C_A / 4 = (0×1 + (−2)(−1) + 2×1 + 0×(−1)) / 4 = 4 / 4 = +1` (Bit `1` recovered cleanly!).
