📖 Chapter 1: Business Communication — 7 Cs of Business Communication, Shannon-Weaver Model, Barriers & Grapevine Networks

4 Questions in Booklet
📌 1.1 7 Cs of Business Communication, Shannon-Weaver Model, Barriers & Grapevine Networks
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Shannon-Weaver Model (1948): Information Source -> Transmitter (Encoder) -> Channel (+ Noise) -> Receiver (Decoder) -> Destination (+ Feedback added by Schramm)
Simple Exam Points
  • The 7 Cs of Effective Business Communication (Cutlip & Center): Clear, Concise, Concrete, Correct, Coherent, Complete, and Courteous.
  • Formal Organizational Communication Flows: Downward (orders, policies, instructions from superior to subordinate), Upward (feedback, grievances, progress reports from subordinate to superior), Horizontal / Lateral (peer-to-peer coordination across same hierarchy level), and Diagonal / Crosswise (between different levels and departments without reporting line).
  • Informal Communication ('Grapevine' — Keith Davis 4 Chains): Single Strand Chain, Gossip Chain (1 person tells everyone), Probability Chain (random), and **Cluster Chain** (MOST COMMON in organizations — teller shares with select trusted group!).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Four Communication Barriers: (1) Semantic Barriers (badly expressed message, symbols with multiple meanings, faulty translation, technical jargon), (2) Psychological Barriers (premature evaluation, lack of attention, distrust), (3) Organizational Barriers (rigid rules, status hierarchy), (4) Personal Barriers.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: Informal Communication (Grapevine) ka sabse common pattern **Cluster Chain** hota hai! Aur Encoding/Decoding me words ke galat arth ya technical jargon ki wajah se aane wali rukawat ko **Semantic Barrier** kehte hain!

📌 2.1 Management Functions (POSDCORB), Fayol's 14 Principles, Maslow, Herzberg & SWOT / BCG Matrix
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Luther Gulick POSDCORB: Planning, Organizing, Staffing, Directing, Coordinating, Reporting, Budgeting
  • 🔹 BCG Matrix Axes: Relative Market Share (X) vs Market Growth Rate (Y)
Simple Exam Points
  • Fayol's 14 Principles of Management vs Taylor's Scientific Management: **Unity of Command** means each subordinate receives orders from and is accountable to ONLY ONE superior (prevents dual subordination), whereas **Unity of Direction** means 'One Head and One Plan' for a group of activities having the same objective!
  • **Gang Plank** breaks the rigid Scalar Chain for urgent horizontal communication between same-level employees.
  • Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory separates **Hygiene / Maintenance Factors** (salary, company policy, working conditions, job security — their absence causes dissatisfaction, but their presence only prevents dissatisfaction) from **Motivational Factors** (achievement, recognition, responsibility, growth — create true satisfaction).
  • BCG Portfolio Matrix classifies SBUs into: **Stars** (High Growth, High Share), **Cash Cows** (Low Growth, High Share — milked for cash), **Question Marks / Problem Children** (High Growth, Low Share), and **Dogs** (Low Growth, Low Share).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs (Bottom to Top): (1) Physiological Needs -> (2) Safety/Security Needs -> (3) Social/Belongingness Needs -> (4) Esteem Needs -> (5) Self-Actualization Needs.

📖 Chapter 3: Economics & Banking — Microeconomics — Elasticity of Demand, Market Structures, Cost Curves & Break-Even Analysis

4 Questions in Booklet
📌 3.1 Microeconomics — Elasticity of Demand, Market Structures, Cost Curves & Break-Even Analysis
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Break-Even Point (Units) = Fixed Cost / (Selling Price per unit − Variable Cost per unit) = FC / Contribution
  • 🔹 Price Elasticity E_d = (%ΔQ / %ΔP) = (dQ/dP) × (P/Q)
Simple Exam Points
  • Market Structures Comparison: (1) Perfect Competition: Large number of buyers/sellers, homogeneous product, free entry/exit, firm is a **Price Taker** facing a perfectly elastic horizontal demand curve (`P = AR = MR`, `E_d = ∞`), equilibrium where `MR = MC` and MC cuts MR from below
  • (2) Monopoly: Single seller, no close substitutes, blocked entry, **Price Maker**, `AR > MR` (for linear demand, slope of MR is twice the slope of AR)
  • (3) Monopolistic Competition (Chamberlin): Differentiated products, selling/advertising costs, excess capacity
  • (4) Oligopoly (Paul Sweezy): Few interdependent giant sellers, **Kinked Demand Curve** explaining price rigidity!
  • Cost Relationships: When Marginal Cost (MC) < Average Cost (AC), AC falls
  • when MC > AC, AC rises
  • **MC always intersects both AVC and AC at their respective MINIMUM points**!
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Break-Even Numerical: A software firm has Fixed Costs = ₹2,40,000, Selling Price per license = ₹1,000, and Variable Cost per license = ₹400. Unit Contribution = 1,000 − 400 = ₹600. Break-Even Point = 2,40,000 / 600 = 400 licenses (P/V Ratio = 600/1000 = 60%).
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: Break-Even Point (BEP) par Total Revenue = Total Cost (No Profit, No Loss), aur `Contribution per unit = Selling Price − Variable Cost`! Perfect Competition me `P = AR = MR` hota hai, aur Oligopoly me **Kinked Demand Curve (Paul Sweezy)** banta hai.

📖 Chapter 4: Financial Accounting — Financial Accounting — Three Golden Rules, GAAP Concepts, Trial Balance, Final Accounts & Ratios

10 Questions in Booklet
📌 4.1 Financial Accounting — Three Golden Rules, GAAP Concepts, Trial Balance, Final Accounts & Ratios
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Accounting Equation: Assets = Liabilities + Capital (Owner's Equity)
  • 🔹 Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities (Ideal 2 : 1)
  • 🔹 Quick / Acid-Test Ratio = (Current Assets − Inventory − Prepaid Expenses) / Current Liabilities (Ideal 1 : 1)
Simple Exam Points
  • Three Golden Rules of Double-Entry Bookkeeping (Luca Pacioli): (1) **Personal Account** (Natural, Artificial, Representative — e.g., Ram's A/c, Bank A/c, Outstanding Salary A/c!): *Debit the Receiver, Credit the Giver*
  • (2) **Real Account** (Tangible & Intangible Assets — e.g., Cash, Machinery, Goodwill, Patents, Trademarks): *Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out*
  • (3) **Nominal Account** (Expenses, Losses, Incomes, Gains — e.g., Salary, Rent, Commission, Discount): *Debit all expenses and losses, Credit all incomes and gains*.
  • Note: Whenever a prefix/suffix (`Outstanding`, `Prepaid`, `Accrued`, `Unearned`) is added to a Nominal Account, it immediately becomes a **Representative Personal Account**!
  • Accounting Cycle: Source Voucher -> Journal (Book of Original Entry) -> Ledger (Book of Final/Principal Entry) -> Trial Balance -> Trading & P&L Account -> Balance Sheet.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Gross Profit & COGS Math: Opening Stock = ₹40,000, Net Purchases = ₹1,50,000, Direct Wages/Carriage Inwards = ₹20,000, Closing Stock = ₹50,000, Net Sales = ₹2,20,000. Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) = Opening Stock + Net Purchases + Direct Expenses − Closing Stock = 40,000 + 1,50,000 + 20,000 − 50,000 = ₹1,60,000. Gross Profit = Sales − COGS = 2,20,000 − 1,60,000 = ₹60,000.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: `Salary Account` ek **Nominal Account** hai, par `Outstanding Salary` ya `Prepaid Insurance` ke aage prefix lagte hi wo **Representative Personal Account** ban jata hai! Aur `Goodwill / Patent / Copyright` **Intangible Real Accounts** hain!

📖 Chapter 6: Electronic Payment Systems — Electronic Payment Systems — RTGS, NEFT, IMPS, UPI, SET Protocol, PCI-DSS & 3D-Secure

📌 6.1 Electronic Payment Systems — RTGS, NEFT, IMPS, UPI, SET Protocol, PCI-DSS & 3D-Secure
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 RTGS Minimum = ₹2,00,000 (Max = No Limit, Real-Time Gross Settlement, 24×7×365)
  • 🔹 NEFT Minimum = No Minimum (Half-hourly DNS batches, 24×7×365)
  • 🔹 IMPS / UPI = Instant 24×7 via NPCI
Simple Exam Points
  • Indian Digital Payment Infrastructure: (1) **RTGS** (managed by RBI): Real-Time (instant) and Gross (settled instruction-by-instruction without netting) for high-value transactions >= ₹2 Lakh
  • (2) **NEFT** (managed by RBI): Deferred Net Settlement (DNS) in 48 half-hourly batches around the clock with no minimum/maximum floor
  • (3) **IMPS** & **UPI** (managed by **NPCI** — National Payments Corporation of India, founded 2008 by RBI & IBA): Instant 24×7 retail interbank transfer via Virtual Payment Address (`user@bank`) and 2FA UPI PIN.
  • **SET (Secure Electronic Transaction)** protocol (developed by Visa & Mastercard) uses **Dual Signatures** so the **Merchant sees ONLY the Order Information (OI)** (never the customer's credit card number!) and the **Bank/Acquirer sees ONLY the Payment Information (PI)** (never the itemized items ordered!), linked cryptographically via `H(H(OI) || H(PI))`!
✅ Direct Exam Example: UTR (Unique Transaction Reference) Numbers: RTGS UTR is **22 characters** long (`XXXXRYYYYMMDD#########`), whereas NEFT UTR is **16 characters** long!
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: **SET Protocol** ke **Dual Signature** me Merchant ko customer ka Credit Card number NAHI dikhta (sirf Order Info dikhta hai), aur Bank ko customer ne kya saman kharida wo NAHI dikhta (sirf Payment Info dikhta hai)!

📖 Chapter 7: Knowledge Management — Knowledge Management (KM), DIKW Pyramid, Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge & Nonaka-Takeuchi SECI Model

2 Questions in Booklet
📌 7.1 Knowledge Management (KM), DIKW Pyramid, Tacit vs Explicit Knowledge & Nonaka-Takeuchi SECI Model
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 DIKW Hierarchy: Data (Raw facts) -> Information (Contextualized data) -> Knowledge (Actionable patterns/experience) -> Wisdom (Foresight/Judgment)
  • 🔹 SECI: Socialization -> Externalization -> Combination -> Internalization
Simple Exam Points
  • Polanyi's Knowledge Classification: **Explicit Knowledge** is codified, documented, formal, and easily stored in databases/manuals/SOPs
  • **Tacit Knowledge** is personal, intuitive, experiential know-how residing inside human minds (hard to formalize or transfer).
  • Nonaka and Takeuchi's **SECI Spiral Model (1995)** defines 4 modes of knowledge conversion: (1) **Socialization (Tacit -> Tacit)**: Sharing tacit experience through direct observation, apprenticeship, mentoring, and brainstorming
  • (2) **Externalization (Tacit -> Explicit)**: Articulating tacit insights into explicit metaphors, concepts, manuals, or expert rules
  • (3) **Combination (Explicit -> Explicit)**: Synthesizing, sorting, and combining multiple explicit documents/databases into a new report or knowledge base
  • (4) **Internalization (Explicit -> Tacit)**: 'Learning by doing'—reading explicit manuals and practicing until it becomes ingrained tacit skill.
✅ Direct Exam Example: KM System Architecture: Knowledge Discovery (Data Mining) -> Knowledge Capture (Interviews/Externalization) -> Knowledge Sharing (Communities of Practice, Wikis) -> Knowledge Application (Case-Based Reasoning, Expert Systems).
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: Nonaka-Takeuchi **SECI Model** 100% exam me aata hai: **S**ocialization = Tacit to Tacit; **E**xternalization = Tacit to Explicit (likhna); **C**ombination = Explicit to Explicit (jodna); **I**nternalization = Explicit to Tacit (seekh kar aadat banana)!
