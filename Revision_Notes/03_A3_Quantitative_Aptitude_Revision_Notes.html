📖 Chapter 1: Solid Mensuration 2D/3D: 3D Solid Mensuration — Cylinder, Cone, Sphere, Hemisphere

13 Questions in Booklet
📌 1.1 3D Solid Mensuration — Cylinder, Cone, Sphere, Hemisphere & Frustum
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Cylinder Vol = πr²h, CSA = 2πrh
  • 🔹 Cone Vol = (1/3)πr²h, l = √(r²+h²), CSA = πrl
  • 🔹 Sphere Vol = (4/3)πr³, SA = 4πr²
  • 🔹 Hemisphere Vol = (2/3)πr³, CSA = 2πr², TSA = 3πr²
Simple Exam Points
  • When a 3D metallic solid (e.g., Sphere) is melted and recast into N smaller solids (e.g., Cones or Cylinders) or drawn into a long cylindrical wire, Total Volume remains strictly invariant: V_initial = N × V_final.
  • For a Frustum of a Cone (radii R and r, height h, slant height l = √(h² + (R−r)²)): Volume = (1/3)πh(R² + r² + Rr) and CSA = π(R + r)l.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: A solid metallic sphere of radius 6 cm is melted and drawn into a cylindrical wire of radius 0.2 cm. Find length L of wire: (4/3)π(6)³ = π(0.2)² × L => 288 = 0.04 × L => L = 7200 cm = 72 meters.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: Solid Hemisphere ka Total Surface Area (TSA) 2πr² nahi balki 3πr² hota hai (2πr² curved + πr² flat circular base)! Aur Cylinder, Hemisphere, Cone agar same base radius r aur height h = r ke hon, to unke Volumes ka ratio 3 : 2 : 1 hota hai!
📌 1.2 2D Mensuration, Equilateral Triangle, Rhombus, Trapezium & Inscribed Circles
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Equilateral Δ: Area = (√3/4)a², h = (√3/2)a, Inradius r = a/(2√3), Circumradius R = a/√3 (R : r = 2 : 1, Area R:r = 4 : 1)
  • 🔹 Rhombus Area = (1/2)d1·d2 with a² = (d1/2)² + (d2/2)²
Simple Exam Points
  • In any Rhombus, diagonals bisect each other at right angles (90°), forming four congruent right-angled triangles with hypotenuse equal to side 'a' and legs d1/2 and d2/2.
  • For a Room of dimensions L × B × H: Area of 4 Walls = 2(L + B)H and Length of Longest Rod (Space Diagonal) = √(L² + B² + H²).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: The diagonals of a rhombus are 16 cm and 30 cm. Find its area and perimeter. Area = (1/2) × 16 × 30 = 240 cm². Half-diagonals are 8 cm and 15 cm -> Side a = √(8² + 15²) = 17 cm -> Perimeter = 4 × 17 = 68 cm.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: Equilateral Triangle me Circumradius (R) aur Inradius (r) ka ratio hamesha 2 : 1 hota hai, isliye Circumcircle aur Incircle ke Area ka ratio πR² : πr² = 4 : 1 hota hai!

📖 Chapter 2: Profit, Loss & Discount: Profit, Loss, Marked Price, Successive Discounts

13 Questions in Booklet
📌 2.1 Profit, Loss, Marked Price, Successive Discounts & MP/CP Ratio
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 MP / CP = (100 + Profit%) / (100 − Discount%)
  • 🔹 Equivalent Single Discount for d1%, d2% = d1 + d2 − (d1·d2)/100
Simple Exam Points
  • The golden bridge equation linking Cost Price (CP) and Marked Price (MP) when a trader allows a discount of D% on MP and still earns a profit of P% on CP is directly derived from SP = MP(100−D)/100 = CP(100+P)/100, giving MP : CP = (100 + P) : (100 − D).
  • If two articles are sold at the SAME Selling Price (SP_1 = SP_2), one at x% profit and the other at x% loss, there is ALWAYS a net loss of (x² / 100)%.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: A shopkeeper gives a 15% discount on Marked Price and still earns a 19% profit. What is MP : CP? MP / CP = (100 + 19) / (100 − 15) = 119 / 85 = 7 / 5 (MP is 40% above CP).
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: MP aur CP ka direct ratio formula: MP / CP = (100 + Profit%) / (100 − Discount%) — is ek formula se DSSSB ke 80% discount सवाल 5 second me hal hote hain!
📌 2.2 Dishonest Dealer False Weight & Buy-X-Get-Y Free Schemes
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Dishonest Dealer Gain% = [(True Weight − False Weight) / False Weight] × 100
  • 🔹 'Buy X Get Y Free' Discount% = [Y / (X + Y)] × 100
Simple Exam Points
  • In dishonest shopkeeper problems, always treat the actual weight given to the customer (False Weight, e.g., 800 g instead of 1000 g) as the Cost Outflow (CP base = 800) and the weight charged to the customer as the Revenue Inflow (SP base = 1000).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: A dishonest grocer professes to sell sugar at 10% profit, but uses a weight of 800 g instead of 1 kg. Effective CP = 800; Charged SP = 1000 × 1.10 = 1100. Net Profit% = [(1100 − 800) / 800] × 100 = 37.5%.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: 'Buy 5 Get 3 Free' me Discount% = (3/5)×100 = 60% MAT kar dena! Dukan se kul (5 + 3 = 8) items gaye hain, isliye Discount% = (3 / 8) × 100 = 37.5% hoga!

📖 Chapter 3: Ratio, Proportion & Percentage — Percentage Successive Change, Price-Consumption Product Constancy & Alligation

19 Questions in Booklet
📌 3.1 Percentage Successive Change, Price-Consumption Product Constancy & Alligation
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Successive Net % Change = A + B + (A·B)/100
  • 🔹 If Price rises by R/D fraction, Consumption must fall by R/(D+R) for constant expenditure
  • 🔹 Alligation Ratio = (Dearer − Mean) : (Mean − Cheaper)
Simple Exam Points
  • Whenever Expenditure = Price × Consumption (or Area = Length × Breadth) is kept constant, Price and Consumption are inversely proportional: if Price increases by 25% (+1/4), Consumption must decrease by 1/(4+1) = 1/5 = 20%.
  • For vessel replacement of milk (initial volume V, replacing x litres n times with water), Pure Milk Remaining = V × (1 − x/V)ⁿ.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: From a 60-litre cask of pure milk, 12 litres (1/5th) is drawn out and replaced with water, and this process is repeated a second time. Pure milk left = 60 × (1 − 12/60)² = 60 × (4/5)² = 60 × 16/25 = 38.4 litres.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: Fraction Table yaad rakhein: +1/4 (+25%) ko balance karne ke liye −1/5 (−20%) chahiye; +1/5 (+20%) ko balance karne ke liye −1/6 (−16.67%) chahiye!

📖 Chapter 4: Number System & Algebra — Divisibility Rules (8, 9, 11, 72, 88, 99), Remainder Theorem & Unit Digits

📌 4.1 Divisibility Rules (8, 9, 11, 72, 88, 99), Remainder Theorem & Unit Digits
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Divisibility by 72 = Div by 8 (last 3 digits) AND Div by 9 (digit sum)
  • 🔹 Divisibility by 88 = Div by 8 AND 11
  • 🔹 Number of Factors of N = p^a·q^b·r^c is (a+1)(b+1)(c+1)
Simple Exam Points
  • For an 8-or-10 digit number 5432x78y divisible by 72: Step 1: Apply Divisibility by 8 on the last three digits '78y' to find y (784 is divisible by 8 -> y = 4).
  • Step 2: Apply Divisibility by 9 (sum of all digits divisible by 9) to find x!
  • Also remember: LCM × HCF = Product of Two Numbers (N1 × N2)
  • for fractions, HCF = HCF(Numerators)/LCM(Denominators) and LCM = LCM(Numerators)/HCF(Denominators).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: Find the remainder when 2^68 is divided by 65. Since 2^6 = 64 ≡ −1 (mod 65), write 2^68 = (2^6)^11 × 2^2 ≡ (−1)^11 × 4 = −4 ≡ 65 − 4 = 61.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: Divisibility by 88 ya 72 me hamesha PEHLE 8 ka rule (last 3 digits) lagakar 'y' nikalein, aur uske baad 9 ya 11 ka rule lagakar 'x' nikalein!

📖 Chapter 5: Time, Speed & Distance — Harmonic Average Speed, Relative Speed of Trains & Boat-Stream Equations

14 Questions in Booklet
📌 5.1 Harmonic Average Speed, Relative Speed of Trains & Boat-Stream Equations
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Equal-Distance Average Speed = 2xy / (x + y)
  • 🔹 km/hr to m/s: ×(5/18)
  • 🔹 m/s to km/hr: ×(18/5)
  • 🔹 Boat Speed u = (D + U)/2, Stream Speed v = (D − U)/2
Simple Exam Points
  • When two trains of lengths L1 and L2 move at speeds S1 and S2: Time to cross each other = (L1 + L2) / S_rel, where S_rel = (S1 + S2) in opposite directions and |S1 − S2| in the same direction.
  • If two trains start simultaneously and reach their destinations in t1 and t2 hours AFTER meeting each other en route, then S1 / S2 = √(t2 / t1).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: A 180 m long train running at 72 km/hr crosses a platform in 25 seconds. Speed = 72 × (5/18) = 20 m/s. Total distance covered = 20 × 25 = 500 m. Platform length = 500 − 180 = 320 m.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: Crossing ke baad manzil par pahunchne ka time t1 aur t2 diya ho, to Speed Ratio S1 : S2 = √t2 : √t1 hota hai (aur Meeting Time T = √(t1 × t2))!

📖 Chapter 6: Simple & Compound Interest — Compound Interest vs Simple Interest (2-Year & 3-Year Difference Shortcuts)

13 Questions in Booklet
📌 6.1 Compound Interest vs Simple Interest (2-Year & 3-Year Difference Shortcuts)
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 2-Yr (CI − SI) = P(R/100)²
  • 🔹 3-Yr (CI − SI) = P(R/100)² × (3 + R/100)
  • 🔹 Ratio (CI_2yr / SI_2yr) = (200 + R) / 200
Simple Exam Points
  • In Simple Interest, interest is constant every year, whereas in Compound Interest, interest earns interest on previous years' interest.
  • Effective 2-year CI rate at R% p.a.
  • is (2R + R²/100)% (e.g., at 10%, SI=20%, CI=21%
  • at 8%, SI=16%, CI=16.64%).
  • For 3 years at 10%, SI=30% and CI=33.1% (Difference = 3.1% of P).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: The difference between CI and SI on a sum for 3 years at 10% p.a. is ₹930. Since 3-year difference at 10% is 3.1% of P: 0.031 × P = 930 => Principal P = ₹30,000.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: Agar CI par koi dhanrashi t saal me k guna (k times) hoti hai, to k^n guna hone me t × n saal lagenge (jaise 4 saal me 2 guna -> 8 guna = 2³ hone me 4 × 3 = 12 saal lagenge)!

📖 Chapter 7: Time & Work & Pipes — Time & Work LCM Efficiency Method, Leaving Workers & M1·D1·H1 / W1

11 Questions in Booklet
📌 7.1 Time & Work LCM Efficiency Method, Leaving Workers & M1·D1·H1 / W1
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Total Work W = LCM(d1, d2, d3)
  • 🔹 Daily Efficiency E_i = W / d_i
  • 🔹 Chain Rule: (M1 × D1 × H1) / W1 = (M2 × D2 × H2) / W2
Simple Exam Points
  • Shortcut Rules for Leaving Workers: (1) If worker A leaves 'x' days AFTER starting, subtract A's x-day work from Total Work
  • (2) If worker B leaves 'y' days BEFORE the completion of work, ADD B's y-day work to Total Work and divide by the combined efficiency of all workers!
✅ Direct Exam Example: Worked Example: A and B can do a work in 12 and 15 days respectively (LCM = 60 units; A = 5 u/day, B = 4 u/day). They start together, but A leaves 3 days before completion. Adjusted Total Work = 60 + 3(5) = 75 units. Total days = 75 / (5 + 4) = 75/9 = 8⅓ days.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: 'Kaam khatam hone se y din PEHLE chhod kar gaya' -> us vyakti se y din zabardasti aur kaam karwa lo (Total Work me uska y-day kaam ADD kar do) aur sabकी combined efficiency se bhaag de do!

(A) 7 days

(C) 6 and 2/3 days (20/3 days) ✅
(D) 7.5 days
Answer (C): LCM(12, 15) = 60 units. A = 5 u/day, B = 4 u/day. Together = 60 / (5 + 4) = 60 / 9 = 20/3 = 6 2/3 days.
Q7.2: A and B together can do a work in 8 days, while A alone can do it in 12 days. In how many days can B alone finish the work?
(A) 16 days
(B) 20 days
(C) 18 days
(D) 24 days ✅
Answer (D): B alone = (12 × 8) / (12 − 8) = 96 / 4 = 24 days.
Q7.3: 15 men working 8 hours a day can build a wall in 10 days. How many days will 12 men working 10 hours a day take to build the same wall?
(A) 10 days ✅
(B) 12 days
(C) 8 days
(D) 15 days
Answer (A): M1 D1 H1 = M2 D2 H2 => 15 × 10 × 8 = 12 × D2 × 10 => D2 = 1200 / 120 = 10 days.
Q7.4: A is twice as efficient as B, and together they finish a work in 14 days. In how many days can A alone finish the work?
(A) 28 days
(B) 21 days ✅
(C) 42 days
(D) 18 days
Answer (B): Efficiency A:B = 2:1 (sum = 3). Total work = 3 × 14 = 42 units. A alone = 42 / 2 = 21 days.
Q7.5: Pipe A can fill a tank in 10 hours and Pipe B can empty the full tank in 15 hours. If both pipes are opened together, in how many hours will the tank be filled?
(A) 25 hours
(B) 20 hours
(C) 30 hours ✅
(D) 12 hours
Answer (C): Net filling rate = (1/10) − (1/15) = (3 − 2)/30 = 1/30 => 30 hours.
