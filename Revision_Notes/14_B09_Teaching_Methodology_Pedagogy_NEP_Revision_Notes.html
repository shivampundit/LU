📌 1.1 Bloom's Taxonomy (1956 vs 2001 Revised Anderson-Krathwohl) & Constructivist Learning Theories
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 1956 Bloom (Nouns): Knowledge -> Comprehension -> Application -> Analysis -> Synthesis -> Evaluation
  • 🔹 2001 Revised (Verbs): Remember -> Understand -> Apply -> Analyze -> Evaluate -> Create
Simple Exam Points
  • Bloom's 3 Domains of Learning (3 H's): Cognitive Domain (Head — Bloom, 1956
  • Revised by Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001 where 'Creating' became the highest HOTS level above 'Evaluating'), Affective Domain (Heart/Attitudes — Krathwohl: Receiving, Responding, Valuing, Organizing, Characterizing), Psychomotor Domain (Hand/Motor skills — Simpson/Harrow/Dave: Imitation, Manipulation, Precision, Articulation, Naturalization).
  • Constructivism: Jean Piaget (Cognitive Constructivism — Schema, Assimilation, Accommodation, 4 Stages: Sensorimotor 0-2, Preoperational 2-7, Concrete Operational 7-11, Formal Operational 11+ where abstract algorithmic/hypothetical-deductive thinking emerges!) vs Lev Vygotsky (Social Constructivism — Zone of Proximal Development ZPD, Scaffolding, More Knowledgeable Other MKO).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Three Levels of Teaching: (1) Memory Level (Herbart — rote recall, stimulus-response, lowest thoughtless level), (2) Understanding Level (Morrison — comprehension of principles, relationships, and generalizations), (3) Reflective Level (Hunt — highest problem-centric, critical/creative hypothesis testing level).
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: 1956 ke Original Bloom Taxonomy me sabse upar 'Evaluation' tha, par 2001 ke Revised Bloom Taxonomy (Anderson & Krathwohl) me Verbs use kiye gaye aur 'Creating' (Nirman karna) sabse highest level ban gaya!

Q1.1: Who propounded the three hierarchical levels of teaching: Memory Level, Understanding Level, and Reflective Level respectively?
(A) Herbart, Morrison, and Hunt ✅
(B) Piaget, Vygotsky, and Bruner
(C) Bloom, Anderson, and Krathwohl
(D) Skinner, Pavlov, and Thorndike
Answer (A): Herbart (Memory Level), Morrison (Understanding Level), and Hunt (Reflective Level) — Memory Level (least thoughtful, rote recall) was given by Johann Friedrich Herbart, Understanding Level by H.C. Morrison, and Reflective Level (highest, problem-solving) by Hunt.

Answer (B): Pre-active Phase (Planning), Interactive Phase (Classroom Execution), and Post-active Phase (Evaluation) — Jackson's 3 phases of teaching are: Pre-active (lesson planning before entering class), Interactive (actual classroom interaction), and Post-active (testing and feedback).

(A) From Abstract to Concrete
(B) From Complex to Simple
(C) From Concrete to Abstract ✅
(D) From Unknown to Known
Answer (C): From Concrete to Abstract (and From Whole to Part) — Showing the visible output first and then analyzing abstract tags follows the pedagogical maxim 'From Concrete to Abstract'.

(A) Principal, Teacher, and Parents
(B) Blackboard, Textbook, and Examination
(C) Memory, Understanding, and Reflection
(D) Teacher, Learner (Student), and Curriculum / Social Environment ✅
Answer (D): John Dewey expanded John Adams' Bipolar model (Teacher & Student) into a Tripolar process comprising Teacher, Student, and Curriculum/Environment.
Q1.5: In the Revised Bloom's Taxonomy (2001) by Lorin Anderson and David Krathwohl, what is the correct ascending order of the six cognitive process dimensions from lowest to highest?
(A) Remember -> Understand -> Apply -> Analyze -> Evaluate -> Create ✅

📌 2.1 Edgar Dale's Cone of Experience, Micro-Teaching Cycle (NCERT 36 Min) & Lesson Planning
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 NCERT Indian Micro-Teaching Cycle (36 Minutes): Teach (6m) -> Feedback (6m) -> Re-plan (12m) -> Re-teach (6m) -> Re-feedback (6m) [Total = 36 Mins]
Simple Exam Points
  • Edgar Dale's Cone of Experience arranges Teaching-Learning Materials (TLM) from most concrete/experiential at the base (Direct Purposeful Experiences, Contrived Simulations, Dramatized Experiences — ~90% retention) to most abstract at the apex (Verbal Symbols / Text / Lecture — ~10% retention).
  • Micro-Teaching (coined by Dwight W.
  • Allen at Stanford, 1963) is a scaled-down teacher training technique (5–10 pupils, 5–10 minutes, practicing ONE specific teaching skill at a time).
  • Lesson Planning Frameworks: Herbartian 5/6 Steps (Preparation -> Presentation -> Association/Comparison -> Generalization -> Application -> Recapitulation) vs 5E Constructivist Model (Roger Bybee: Engage -> Explore -> Explain -> Elaborate -> Evaluate).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Dale's Cone Rule: Moving from Base to Pinnacle (Bottom to Top) of Dale's Cone increases ABSTRACTNESS and decreases sensory concreteness.
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: Micro-Teaching (Dwight Allen) ka NCERT standard Indian cycle 36 minutes ka hota hai: Teach(6) + Feedback(6) + Re-plan(12) + Re-teach(6) + Re-feedback(6) = 36 minutes (sirf Re-plan 12 min ka hota hai, baaki charo 6-6 min ke)!

📖 Chapter 3: Assessment & Evaluation — Formative vs Summative Evaluation, Assessment Of/For/As Learning & Test Quality Metrics

21 Questions in Booklet
📌 3.1 Formative vs Summative Evaluation, Assessment Of/For/As Learning & Test Quality Metrics
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 Assessment FOR Learning = Formative (During instruction, diagnostic feedback)
  • 🔹 Assessment OF Learning = Summative (End-of-term grading/certification)
  • 🔹 Assessment AS Learning = Self/Peer Metacognitive monitoring
Simple Exam Points
  • Types of Educational Evaluation (Scriven & Bloom): (1) Placement Evaluation (entry behavior check before instruction, e.g., entrance/readiness test), (2) Formative Evaluation (continuous monitoring during teaching-learning to improve learning via feedback), (3) Diagnostic Evaluation (identifies persistent underlying root causes of specific learning difficulties, followed by Remedial Teaching), (4) Summative Evaluation (terminal judgment at the end of a course).
  • Norm-Referenced Test (NRT — compares a student's percentile rank against peers, e.g., DSSSB merit list) vs Criterion-Referenced Test (CRT — measures mastery against a fixed learning standard/cutoff regardless of peers).
  • Four Characteristics of a Good Test: Validity (measures what it claims to measure), Reliability (consistency/reproducibility of scores across administrations), Objectivity (free from examiner bias), and Usability.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Validity vs Reliability Rule: A Valid test is ALWAYS Reliable, whereas a Reliable test is NOT necessarily Valid (e.g., a broken weighing scale that consistently adds +5 kg is 100% Reliable, but Invalid!).
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: 'Assessment FOR Learning' = Formative (padhaai ke dauran); 'Assessment OF Learning' = Summative (ant me result/grade); 'Assessment AS Learning' = Jab bacha khud apna self-assessment (Metacognition) kare!

📌 4.1 Inclusive Education, RPWD Act 2016 (21 Disabilities), UDL & Specific Learning Disabilities
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 RPWD Act 2016 replaced PWD Act 1995: Expanded recognized disabilities from 7 to 21
  • 🔹 Benchmark Disability >= 40%
  • 🔹 Govt Job Reservation = 4%, Higher Ed Reservation = 5%
Simple Exam Points
  • Inclusive Education educates Children With Special Needs (CWSN) alongside non-disabled peers under the same roof by adapting the school system, curriculum, and pedagogy through Universal Design for Learning (UDL: Multiple Means of Representation, Action/Expression, and Engagement).
  • Specific Learning Disabilities (SLDs): Dyslexia (Reading & phonological decoding disorder), Dysgraphia (Handwriting & fine-motor writing disorder), Dyscalculia (Mathematical number sense & arithmetic calculation disorder), Dyspraxia (Motor coordination disorder), Aphasia/Dysphasia (Spoken language comprehension disorder), ADHD (Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder).
  • For visually impaired CS students, assistive tech includes Screen Readers (JAWS, NVDA, Orca), Refreshable Braille Displays, and Tactile Graphics.
✅ Direct Exam Example: Exclusion vs Segregation vs Integration vs Inclusion: Segregation places CWSN in separate special schools; Integration places CWSN in mainstream schools expecting the child to adjust to the rigid school; Inclusion transforms the school environment, TLM, and assessment to fit every child!
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: Dyslexia = Reading disability; Dysgraphia = Writing disability; Dyscalculia = Math/Calculation disability! Aur RPWD Act 2016 me disabilities ki sankhya 7 se badhakar 21 kar di gayi hai.

📖 Chapter 5: CS Teaching Methods — Computer Science Pedagogy — Pair Programming, Computational Thinking, PRIMM & Inductive/Deductive

16 Questions in Booklet
📌 5.1 Computer Science Pedagogy — Pair Programming, Computational Thinking, PRIMM & Inductive/Deductive
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 4 Pillars of Computational Thinking (Jeannette Wing): Decomposition -> Pattern Recognition -> Abstraction -> Algorithmic Design
  • 🔹 PRIMM: Predict -> Run -> Investigate -> Modify -> Make
Simple Exam Points
  • Effective CS Pedagogical Strategies: (1) Inductive vs Deductive Method: Inductive moves from Specific Examples/Traces -> General Rule/Syntax (discovery-oriented, learner-centric, durable retention, best for introducing loops/recursion)
  • Deductive moves from General Formula/Syntax -> Specific Examples (fast, teacher-centric)
  • (2) Pair Programming in CS Labs: Two students share one workstation — the 'Driver' controls the keyboard/mouse typing code, while the 'Navigator' reviews each line for logic errors and strategic direction, swapping roles every 15–20 minutes
  • (3) Parson's Problems: Scaffolding exercise where learners reorder scrambled lines of correct code (reduces syntax load while testing algorithmic logic)
  • (4) CS Unplugged: Teaching algorithms (binary search, sorting networks, parity bits) through kinesthetic games/cards without computers.
✅ Direct Exam Example: PRIMM Model (Sue Sentance) for Teaching Programming: Before asking novices to write code from scratch, guide them through: Predict (what will this code output?) -> Run (test prediction) -> Investigate (trace variables) -> Modify (tweak functionality) -> Make (build a new program).
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Hinglish Tip: Inductive Method = 'Specific Examples se General Rule ki taraf' (Known to Unknown, Concrete to Abstract); Deductive Method = 'General Rule/Formula se Specific Example ki taraf'! Pair Programming me 'Driver' code type karta hai aur 'Navigator' review karta hai.

📌 6.1 ICT in Education, TPACK Framework, SAMR Model, Flipped Classroom & Govt Digital Initiatives
Key Formulas & Rules
  • 🔹 TPACK (Mishra & Koehler): Technological (TK) ∩ Pedagogical (PK) ∩ Content Knowledge (CK)
  • 🔹 SAMR (Puentedura): Substitution -> Augmentation -> Modification -> Redefinition
Simple Exam Points
  • In the Flipped Classroom model, direct lecture/concept introduction shifts outside class via pre-recorded videos/interactive reading (Lower Bloom levels: Remember/Understand at home), freeing in-class time for collaborative coding, debugging, and project mentoring with the teacher (Higher Bloom levels: Apply/Analyze/Create in class!).
  • Indian Digital Education Initiatives: SWAYAM (MOOC platform built on 4 Quadrants: e-Tutorial Video, e-Content PDF, Discussion Forum, Self-Assessment Quiz), SWAYAM PRABHA (40 DTH TV channels using GSAT-15 satellite broadcasting 24×7 educational lectures), DIKSHA (One Nation One Digital Platform for school teachers/students with QR-coded Energized Textbooks), NISHTHA (teacher training), PARAKH (National Assessment Centre under NEP 2020), Virtual Labs & Spoken Tutorial (IIT Bombay).
✅ Direct Exam Example: Synchronous vs Asynchronous ICT Tools: Synchronous requires real-time simultaneous presence (Live Webinars, Google Meet, Live Chat); Asynchronous allows self-paced time-shifted learning (Recorded MOOCs, Discussion Forums, Email).
💡 Exam Shortcut / Trap: Exam Trap: SWAYAM ke 4 Quadrants hote hain: (1) Video Lecture, (2) Downloadable Reading Text, (3) Self-Assessment Tests/Quizzes, aur (4) Online Discussion Forum! Aur Flipped Classroom me Lecture ghar par aur Homework/Problem-Solving class me hota hai!

Q6.2: In a 'Flipped Classroom' pedagogical model applied to TGT Computer Science, how are direct instruction and hands-on coding reorganized?

Q6.3: In Dr. Ruben Puentedura's 'SAMR Model' for evaluating how technology is integrated into classroom tasks, what do the four levels S-A-M-R stand for from Enhancement (bottom two) to Transformation (top two)?

Answer (A): SAMR levels: Substitution (tech acts as direct tool substitute with no functional change, e.g., typing essay instead of handwriting), Augmentation (functional improvement like spellcheck), Modification (significant task redesign), and Redefinition (creating brand-new learning tasks previously inconceivable).
